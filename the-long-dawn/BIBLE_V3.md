**The grid.** 24 fps, 72 BPM, 4/4. A beat is 20 frames (0.833 s) and a bar is 80 frames (3.333 s); bar *n* starts at
frame (n−1)×80. Frames count from 0 on each cut's own timeline and are half-open: a section runs from f0 up to, not
including, f1, which is the next section's f0. Seconds are frames ÷ 24. "Bar 22 b3.5" is the frame halfway between
beats 3 and 4 of bar 22. Every section starts on a bar line, and shots inside a section may cut on any beat.

## 0. The decision in one page

**Why draft 1 didn't land** (the room agreed on this). Fire meant three things at once (the danger, the alarm and
the reward), so "someone lit a beacon" could not explain why a race stopped. The captions spoke policy while the
pictures spoke myth, so the words felt on the nose and the images vague. The turn had no mechanism. It ran nine ideas
in two minutes. And A, B and C were one plot in three costumes that climaxed on a keynote sunrise from orbit.

**The v3 rule.** One world, three films, three time-scales, three looks. In every film the fire is the gift, the race
is the tragedy, and the way through is to carry it together, in the open, at a pace we can see by. No film says so.

# A · EVERY STEP CLOSER (the allegory)

*Spine: the contrarian. Grafts: the dramatist (the crossing, the rope, the glass lantern, the keeper at the bellows,
the red under the cloud, three lines, and no oaths in A); the essayist (the dead hill, here the dead valley); the poet
(the eyeline chain, the watch tone, the healing tritone, "each saw the others"); the framing critic (the builders'
couplet).*

## A1 · Logline
A new fire, fed on everything we ever wrote, rises at midnight looking like morning. Every step toward it pays in
gold, until the world stands one step from a blaze with no morning after it. Then the rest of us light fires to see
by, and in their light the racers see each other, step back together, and carry the fire across the dark no faster
than its own light allows, into a dawn nobody wins.

## A2 · The central idea, and why it holds
**The same fire is a glare when you race toward it and a dawn when you carry it together.** The fire is never the
villain and is never put out. What changes is how people move around it, and its colour tracks its pace: a cold
white glare when raced toward, the gold of what every step closer pays, the crimson of the race, a warming gold when
carried, and at last the rose-gold of morning. The people's own fires are orange: light to see by.

The turn is caused, step by step:
1. The race runs on two blindnesses (no one can see into the fire; the racers can see the prize but not each other)
   and one incentive (every step closer pays). The brink is where the three meet.
2. A blindness cannot be cured by a stronger forge, only by light that exists to be seen by, lit by people who are
   not racing.
3. Once each racer can see the others, stepping back is no longer stepping back alone, so they stop together.
4. A fire that cannot be unmade and is too precious to lose has to be moved, not quenched, and the only safe speed is
   the speed at which you can see where you put your feet.
5. Where it arrives, it is morning, and the morning is long because the fire was carried.

| key image | what it carries (felt, never said) |
|---|---|
| a cold glow at midnight beyond the ranges, where no sun should rise | the new light arrives as wonder, and as hype |
| towers lit only on the side facing the fire, black to each other | they can see the prize, never each other |
| the crater; the nearest towers gilded as the rim crumbles under them | a bomb that prints gold |
| the keeper at the bellows, who looks for the next tower and sees only glare | afraid, not greedy; none would slow alone |
| one valley: drawn in embers, then ash, then real at dawn | the widest spread of futures there has ever been |
| every ridge catching fire in the same breath | no first fire, no lone hero: the rest of us |
| the towers' backs lit warm; their shutters opening | the work comes out of the dark |
| a lantern of iron and glass; a roped line on a knife-edge that never walks faster than its light | together, in the open, at a pace we can see by |
| the fire's gold and the sun's rim becoming one light | the fire's promise, shared by everyone |

**Look and motion (A's own film).** EMBERS AND BLACK: particles in true black, bloom, streaks and shutter blur; the
physical world appears only as islands of fire until the fire is carried out of the abstraction and into a real
landscape. **Two speeds:** before the grasp the camera races toward the fire against the glare, cutting on the beat
while the rhythm doubles; after the black it never gets ahead of a fire's light. After the turn every cut is
motivated by someone seeing a fire, so the edit itself becomes a beacon chain. Type: Cormorant italic, lower third,
one weight.

## A3 · Runtime: 5:10 (93 bars at 72 BPM)

pace the film argues for; longer turns an argument into a sermon.

# B · THE KEEPER (the wordless legend)

*Spine: the contrarian (the kept fire; the lifetime in one frame; the child who grows up to answer). Opening and
dawn: the dramatist (dusk, the climb, the dead ember, the hand-back). Grafts: the poet (the match cut on one spark;
the child asleep in the scarf; the first birdsong) and the essayist (the open hands). The contrarian's dream prologue
is gone, as both panels asked: B owes A nothing.*

## B1 · Logline
On the longest night the light leaves the mountains. A young woman carries her village's last ember to the summit,
finds it dead, and strikes new fire from stone. She keeps that fire on the longest night of every year for sixty
years, long before anyone answers, until the whole range is alight and the sun comes back to take the fire from her
hands.

## B2 · The central idea, and why it holds
**A fire kept is a fire answered.** B is not an allegory. It is a folk tale about fidelity, and the oldest kind: a
vow, a return every year, a watcher far away who grows up, a gift at the end. It carries the whole film's truth in
time rather than argument: what is carried slowly, hand to hand, in the open, becomes the dawn.

The shape is the dramatist's **three waves of light across one range**, with the second wave stretched across a
life:
1. **Dusk.** The sun leaves the peaks one by one, the lowest first, until her summit holds the last red point.
2. **The fires.** Fires catch on those same peaks one by one, first hers and then, over sixty years, all of them.
3. **Dawn.** The sun returns peak by peak, and each fire pales as the light reaches it, as if handing it back.

The turn is physical and single: the ember she carried is dead, so she must make fire from stone. Everything after
is the answer to that act, and she saves no one: the fire spreads because others come and take it.

| key image | what it carries |
|---|---|
| the light leaving each peak | light is not guaranteed; the night is real |
| the ember greying under her breath | the old fire cannot be carried across; new fire must be made |
| one stone on the cairn at each grey dawn | fidelity without reward, counted |
| a child on a far peak who points; the same peak years later, the child grown, lighting his own | she was seen all along, and the answer grew up |
| travellers who take flame and carry it down, more each year | many hands; the fire spreads at walking pace and no one hoards it |
| village lights under the cloud sea, year by year | flourishing, built by increments |
| each beacon paling as the sun reaches it | the fire's work is done, and it is given back |
| the red scarf around a sleeping child, lit by the sun for the first time | the watch passes on |

**Look and motion (B's own film).** STONE AND SKY: the physical mountain world in natural light, weather and stars;
no embers, no ink, no text, no future sky, no montage of the wider world. Locked frames and slow cranes: time
passes inside the frame, not through cuts; transitions are long dissolves and one match cut on a single spark. The
only red is the scarf, lit by fire until the last shot, when the sun lights it.

## B3 · Runtime: 4:20 (78 bars)
A wordless film holds about four minutes. The lifetime needs about ninety seconds for sixty years to weigh and still
feel like one breath. Dusk and the hand-back each need their whole arc, because the light leaving and returning peak
by peak is the story and cannot be cut into a montage. Past about 4:40 the locked frame would start to feel like a
slideshow.

# C · THE LAST PAGES (the Tolkien cut)

*Spine: the contrarian (the whole life of a Ring, the bearer who cannot let go, the Havens). Frame and pages: the
dramatist (the Red Book by a hearth, the ink pages, burn-throughs that heal, the Deep, the Ring loop that cadences

## C1 · Logline
Told from an old red book: we kindle a fire from every word we ever wrote, and a race with no Dark Lord behind it
beats the fire's light into a Ring. Seized by one hand, the Ring slips away and falls into the snow at the knees of a
young beacon-keeper. Her
fire cannot unmake it, so she calls for aid; the peoples answer hill by hill, and when at the council her hand will
not open, the fire everyone lit takes the Ring from her. The land flowers, she sails into the West, and the book's
last pages are left blank.

## C2 · The central idea, and why it holds
**The fire is not the Ring.** The fire cannot be unmade and should not be: its light could warm every hearth. The
Ring is what the race made of it: the wish to hold that light with one hand, and first. In the old story a Dark Lord
forged the Ring in secret and it could be unmade only in the fire that forged it. In ours there was no Dark Lord,
only every forge in every kingdom, racing. So by the old story's own law, the only fire that can unmake this Ring is
a fire every forge and every people lights together. C does not bend Tolkien; it applies his law honestly to a world
without Sauron, and it keeps his hardest lesson: at the brink no bearer, however good, can let go, and grace has to
come from outside the bearer. Here that grace is everyone.

The chain is forced at every joint: the old ending is closed (*This fire could not be unmade*), so the story needs a
new forge; a Ring abandons every hand, so it will be found again, by someone small; her own fire cannot touch it, as
Frodo's hearth could not, so she calls for aid; the peoples answer and refuse the Ring but keep the fire, slowly; at
the fire she fails, as Isildur and Frodo failed; the fire everyone lit does what Gollum's fall did; and then there is
plenty, and a price, and the pages go on.

## C6 · Why it is neither forced nor on the nose
- **Every reference moves the plot** (the table in C2); nothing is there only to be recognised, and a stranger still
  follows a clear story: a book, a Ring, a finder, a call, a council, a failure, grace, a price.
- **It argues with Tolkien exactly where our world is harder** (no Dark Lord to blame, no forge to go back to) and
  agrees with him where it matters most: no bearer can be trusted at the brink.
- **It takes no side.** The Eye stands over every tower; the fear line is gone before it opens; the ember world cracks
  between every continent; no east or west but the sunrise.
- **The north star is in the book's own shape for hope:** a secret forge answered by a beacon, many peoples, a slow
  council, no single hand, a gift for every hearth.
- **Loving, not parody.** Deeds, objects and shapes instead of names and costumes; the eagles late and far; the
  council in cloaks, not cosplay; short echoes only.

# 7 · Shared world, shared assets

The three films share one mountain world, one heroine, one red scarf, one beacon cairn, the first minute of the
embers act (A and C only), and one motif kit. They diverge by rendering that world three ways:

| | A · night | B · natural light | C · ink and gold |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the mountain world** (`shots/run`) | moonlit night; the red under the cloud (the race, hidden) going out patch by patch; cold-white lanterns warming to gold | the day's own arc: alpenglow leaving, silver moonlight, weather, grey dawns, one gold dawn; no red but the scarf | THE LIVING INK ILLUSTRATION: hatching down the fall line, contours at depth breaks, a parchment sky; fires in full colour with a gold-leaf bloom |
| **the embers act** | crown-less: the fire itself is the prize; the crater; true blacks; the crimson race | none | the Ring, graded gold and brown, the Eye the one saturated red; entered and left through burn-throughs |
| **the heroine's flint take** | one ordinary person among many | a vow after the dead ember | the finder of the Ring |
| **cutting** | hard cuts on the beat, then long takes and eyeline cuts | long dissolves; one match cut on a spark | chapters: page, burn-through, fire |

# 9 · Music brief

**One grid, one kit, three scores.** 24 fps, 72 BPM, one beat = 20 frames, one bar = 80 frames; every section is cut
to whole bars, and the bar maps in the beat sheets are the contract between picture and music. The v2 engine extends
to `timeline_v3_A/B/C` on VSCO-2 CE. The first half's cues become modules that re-flow: the kindling extends by whole
bars, the race's four-bar phrases repeat or drop, the riser keeps its length, the silence stretches.

**The kit:** the Beacon theme (the CALL, D–A–D rising; the ANSWER, falling; a third phrase that comes home); the
thinking fire's glass FM cycle and the IGNITION chord (B♭maj9♯11); the corrupted call (D–A♭–D), which is the race in
A and the Ring in C; the silence piano (D… A… D); the breaths (200 ms before a race entrance, the suck before an
IMPACT, 275 ms before every sunrise).

**Four rules that carry the story, each checkable by analysis:**
1. Whoever lights a fire gets the CALL; only an answer earns the ANSWER (first heard: A at the far world, B at the
   first far pinprick, C in the Run).
2. The whole theme is withheld until each cut's dawn.
3. In A, the thinking cycle's tempo is its pace: sixteenths and faster while raced, quarter notes locked to the bar
   and walking in step once carried.
4. The effects own every fire: no musical stroke on an ignition, a vow, an eagle or a sunrise.

**What the critics earned:** no choir, no organ, no borrowed melodies and nothing Shire-like in C (no whistle, no
pipes); no Mickey-mousing; no cymbal victory; every dawn a bloom out of silence; in A and B the dawn is the warmest
moment, not the loudest.

**A: from thirty-seconds to footsteps** (glass, taiko, dark strings, piano, low brass)

**B: a call without an answer, for sixty years** (a cello, strings, far horns, and wind as the floor; no drums, no
glass, no piano; the quietest score)
- **Dusk:** a low D; the cello steps down one note per peak that goes dark (built from the call turned downward; the
  ANSWER is withheld).
- **The climb, the dead ember:** the cello's last note under the wind, then only wind.
- **The first fire and the first night:** the CALL on the solo cello at the roar; then the call three times, each
  followed by a bar of silence where an answer should be. The audience learns to hear the gap.
- **The lifetime is a chaconne:** one variation over a ground bass for each night we see (every night at first, then
  one in three, then one in five, so the music quickens with the years). While nothing answers the bass steps down a
  degree each time and the harmony darkens. At the first far pinprick it turns and climbs, and the ANSWER is heard
  for the first time, on a distant horn; more voices answer as the torchbearers multiply, until call and answer
  overlap and the bass reaches D major. Each night opens with the three strikes and ends with the stone's knock.
- **The whole range:** horns near and far with call and answer; strings from pp to mf.
- **The hand-back:** a bloom out of silence and the whole theme at last: the CALL late on the cello; the ANSWER
  passed from horn to farther horn, each falling silent as its beacon pales; the home phrase on her cello alone; then
  birdsong; the last chord resolves.

**C: the epic tradition, heard from inside a book** (horns, strings, timpani, a pianissimo brass chorale; original
melodies only)
- **Two sound worlds.** The book is chamber music (cor anglais, harp, clarinet, solo violin, and the pen and page as
  sound); the fire is the orchestra. Every burn-through is a change of orchestration, never a hit.
- **The Ring motif** (the corrupted call, D–A♭–D, turned into a closed loop) never cadences until the Ring is taken
  by the fire, and then resolves into D once. That resolution, not the dawn, is C's emotional climax.
- **Forging to the Eye:** anvil strokes in timpani and low bells, taiko, a glass glitter on each surge; the Deep's
  tick dividing faster; a tam-tam and trombones for the Eye; one held tone and a drop for the Mirror.
- **The finding:** a glass tone falling with the Ring; the motif ppp and sweet in the violas at the find; silence at
  the fist; a lone horn cries the CALL at the roar.
- **The beacons:** driving strings in 3+3+2; horns pass the call peak to peak on beacons 1, 3, 5 and 7; the map in F
  major (violins with a solo violin above, damped harp, an oboe answering).
- **The council:** a clarinet intones the call; the chorale moves in half notes between the vows, never on them;
  under the bearer's fist the motif rises in the low strings, tremolo.
- **The dawn:** a timpani roll that begins in the light; the whole theme in octave violins with a solo violin and
  horns in counterpoint; C's loudest; no crash, no accent for the eagles.
- **The epilogue:** a plain hymn for violas and clarinet; an elegy for strings with the cor anglais carrying the call
  and distant horns answering each coast fire, softer each time; a plagal close under the title.

**Effects that belong to one cut.** A: the gilding's ring, the bellows, the crumbling rim, the hiss of ash, the
lantern's creak and the rope on the arête. B: wind as the floor, the pot's lid, the ember's last hiss, the storm, the
stone's knock each year, and the first birdsong. C: the hearth, page turns, the pen under each ink line, paper
crackle at every burn-through, the Ring's hiss in the snow, the tick of cold metal in fire, the seethe of the melt,
the cock crow, the Eye's long exhale, the sea at the Havens. All cuts: the three flint strikes.

**Deliverables.** `final_A.wav`, `final_B.wav`, `final_C.wav` with linked stems, each exactly its cut's length, at
−16 LUFS integrated and −1.2 dBTP true peak. The composer cannot listen, so the producers are the ears: once at the
animatic stage with the temp score, once on full drafts.

# 11 · Open questions for the producers

**Q1 · Three films, or two?** *Recommendation: three.*
Both panels found two strong, distinct concepts for A and two for B; the overlap was writers reusing A's embers act
as B's first act, and this B has no embers act, no race and no words. A argues, B endures, C remembers.
- **Option 1 (recommended):** A · EVERY STEP CLOSER (5:10), B · THE KEEPER (4:20), C · THE LAST PAGES (6:03), as
  written above. 15:33 in all.
- **Option 2 at its best:** C, plus one longer film, **A/B · THE KEEPER OF THE FIRE** (about 6:30, thirteen lines):

  | t | beat |
  |---|---|
  | 0:00–2:27 | A's first act whole: false dawn, glyphs, the promise, the towers, THE EDGE, the brink, the grasp, the dead valley, the builders' couplet on black (A's T1–T10) |
  | 2:27–2:47 | the stars return; she strikes the first fire and is alone on her summit, the cold glow beyond the ranges: *So someone else lit a fire, to see by.* |
  | 2:47–3:10 | THE FIRST NIGHT: nothing answers; a stone at the grey dawn |
  | 3:10–4:40 | THE LIFETIME: the far child who points; the grown child's beacon; travellers carrying flame down; year by year the chain closes around the glow and the red under the cloud goes out; late in her life, a line of lights walks across the far ridge (THE CROSSING, seen from her summit) |
  | 4:40–5:00 | THE WHOLE RANGE: every summit alight; the glow beyond the ranges gone |
  | 5:00–6:15 | THE HAND-BACK, and the valley below in morning; the child wakes in her scarf. *It took longer than anyone wanted.* · *No one got there first.* |
  | 6:15–6:30 | the title |

  It gains a lot: the lifetime gives *It took longer than anyone wanted* its full weight, there is one score fewer,
  and the slate is about three minutes shorter. It loses more: the wordless legend you asked for; A's cleanest
  mechanism, since "every ridge at once" becomes decades of one keeper, which leans toward a lone hero; and B's dusk
  and dead ember.

**Q2 · B: a lifetime, or one night?** *Recommendation: the lifetime, if its greybox passes at hour 3.*
The alternative is the dramatist's THE LONGEST NIGHT, whole (about 3:40): the same dusk, climb, dead ember and
hand-back, with a thirty-second vigil under a wheeling sky where the sixty years are, and the fire going home in
threads of torches at the end. It is the purest legend in the room and the cheapest score, but gentler and less
moving, and the far child who grows up to answer is lost. If you already know which you prefer, the test only has to
confirm it.

**Q3 · A: no teller, or the festival hill?** *Recommendation: no teller.*
A opens on the false dawn and ends on *No one got there first.* The alternative restores the elder and the child at
dusk, the child's question and the red team's protected last line, *Who lit the first one?*, with the essayist's DEAD
HILL (the same hill, dead, ash falling) as the stakes. It is on disk and it is loved, but B already ends on an old
woman and a child and C is a told story; a third frame of the same kind is the repackaging you flagged.

**Q4 · C: can she let go?** *Recommendation: no.*
She tries her own fire and it cannot touch the Ring; at the council her hand will not open, and the fire everyone
lit takes the Ring from her. That is Tolkien's own law at the Crack of Doom, with the many in Gollum's place, and it
says the thing the vows say: no single hand, however good, can be trusted at the brink. The alternative is the
poet's: she refuses the Ring at once, drops it in her kindling and lights the beacon, *She lit a beacon instead.* It
is the lovelier character beat, but it lets one person's virtue save the day.

**Q5 · How explicit may C be?** *Recommendation: deeds, objects and shapes only.*
No names on screen, an invented script on the Ring, echoes of the book of seven words or fewer ("called for aid",
"not hasty", "the last pages"), no film designs, dialogue or music; and a clearance check with counsel before any
public showing. Each step further is a one-line change, and each is yours to take: the exact quotation, *The last
pages are for you.* (it wants an old hand giving a child the book at the end, as the dramatist staged it); one grey,
wide-brimmed hat seen from above at the council (the story panel's wish, the production panel's cringe risk; test it
as a still); place names written on the ink pages.
