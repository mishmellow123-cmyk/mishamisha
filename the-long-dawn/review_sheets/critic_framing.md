## Short answers to the brief

* **The builders.** Close, but not there yet. "So the companies and the countries raced" is right. The two lines that describe what the builders *wanted* still speak in the builders' own words. They're no longer heroes, but they're still the only people whose inner life the film shows (M1).
* **The turn.** Plausible in shape, and honest enough: "might", "how close we came", fires lit every year, the torch handed down. It needs two small things: an opening line people can actually read, and one admission that it took time (m1, m2).
* **The oaths.** Keep all four as worded. They map onto real proposals. The staging keeps them from reading as a PSA: carved in the stone, no narration, no preamble, and (per the brief; I didn't check the mix) no musical hit on each oath.
* **Cut C.** "This one could not be unmade" is the best line in the three cuts. But C currently takes a side (B1). It also makes the whole technology the One Ring, and Tolkien readers know that teaches the wrong lesson (M3).
* **What would make this room nod.**
  * Report what the builders said and did, not what they felt.
  * One admission that nobody can see inside the fire.
  * A camera that never singles out a country.
  * An ending that hands the watch on. You already have that.

## What already lands (protect it)

* **"So the companies and the countries raced."** It names both actors, casts no villain, and puts the companies first, which is historically right.
* **The race logic as reported speech ("Each said…").** The film never rules on whether it's true. It only shows that everyone said it.
* **"So someone else lit a beacon."** One word, "else", makes the turn come from outside the race. The elder's silence at "Who lit the first one?" keeps it from becoming a lone-hero myth.
* **"This one could not be unmade." (C).** It sits on the one real difference between Tolkien's story and ours, and it's the truest sentence in the film. It echoes Elrond's line in the 2001 film without quoting it, which is fine.
* **The honesty devices.** "Might", "how close we came", fires relit every year (the watch goes on), and the torch handed down. These are why this audience will trust the optimism. The film is neither doomer nor utopian: the dread is earned and the hope is hedged.
* **The first beacon on the roof of the world** (the globe origin is Everest). It's on a border and belongs to no power, and the answer spreads outward from Asia rather than from the West.
* **The future sky** (orbital ring, lights on the Moon). It's faint and unexplained. Keep it that faint; any brighter and it becomes a space-colony poster.

**The oaths, one by one** (as worded in A; for C see M3). Three prohibitions and a promise is the shape of a real bargain.

* **NO SINGLE HAND SHALL HOLD IT.** Against concentration of power: no monopoly, and no company, state or person using it to seize control. Checks and balances. It pays off the grasp.
* **NO FASTER THAN WE CAN SEE.** Pace tied to understanding: if-then commitments, evals, interpretability. "See" has two senses, seeing into it and seeing each other, and both help.
* **NO FORGE IN THE DARK.** Compute governance and verification: no secret training runs, and data centres that are declared and can be inspected. It's the most specific of the four, and the one this audience will quote back to you. In C it is also exact Tolkien: the One Ring was forged in secret.
* **WHAT IT GIVES, IT GIVES TO ALL.** Benefit-sharing. It's the NPT's Article IV bargain: give up the weapon, share the peaceful use. That is also what removes the reason to race.

Protect all four staging conditions (carved, unnarrated, no musical hits, no preamble). In v1, the preamble "They agreed on little — but…" and a bell on each oath were what made this beat read as a PSA. The one thing the oaths never answer is *what if it can't be held?* M2 fixes that earlier in the film, so that oath II ends up answering it.

**On the turn** (ordinary people → the world → a council → the race ends). Plausible in shape. Every real race that ended had three parts:
1. **Outsiders made the danger public.** Russell–Einstein and Pugwash; the baby-tooth survey and Women Strike for Peace; the ozone chemists.
2. **A scare made it vivid.** The Cuban missile crisis came before the 1963 test ban; the ozone hole came before Montreal.
3. **States signed something they could check.**

The film has all three: the beacon; the grasp and the flash ("how close we came"); and the council, with a verification oath. What this room will miss is any sign that it was hard or slow. Every hill answers, one council meets, and "It ended the race." The frame and the coda carry most of the honesty, and m1 and m2 carry the rest. Don't add mechanism, because it's a legend. But don't make it any cleaner either.

## Findings

### BLOCKER

**B1 · Cut C · 745–960 and 1935–2040 · Cut C takes a side: the free world against an Eastern Eye**

* **On screen.**
  * "Each said: if we do not take it, our enemies will." (820–900) is up while the lidless Eye opens (≈836–879).
  * It is still up when the ember globe arrives, framed on East Asia.
  * The globe then splits along a seam that runs from east of Japan past Taiwan and the Philippines, the first island chain.
  * At 1935–2040: "And the free peoples answered."
* **Why this room objects.**
  * In Tolkien, the Eye *is* the Enemy, and the Free Peoples are everyone not under him. Stack the three beats and the reading is instant: the free world against an Eastern Eye. That is the "democratic AI must prevail over authoritarian AI" framing of *Situational Awareness*.
  * The film ends up endorsing the "if we don't take it, our enemies will" logic it set out to show as the trap. That breaks the producers' own rule: no villains, only the natural pull of the race.
  * It loses the people an international-coordination film most needs in the room: Chinese and Global South safety researchers, and anyone who has worked on US–China dialogue.
  * The word "Each" can't keep the line symmetrical against an Eye, a map and a phrase.
* **Change.**
  1. At 1935–2040, replace "And the free peoples answered." with **"And hill by hill, the world answered."** An alternative is "And one by one, the peoples answered."
  2. Move "Each said: if we do not take it, our enemies will." to ≈745–815, over the tower surges where the race logic belongs, so it has faded before the Eye opens. Let the Eye open with no words on screen. With M2's C line it reads as the fire's own will, looking back.
  3. Re-aim the globe (M4).

Don't show C to this audience until all three are done.

### MAJOR

**M1 · Cut A · 820–900 and 1060–1190 · The builders' one moment of silence speaks in their own words**

"Many in the race wanted it to stop. / None of them could stop alone."

1. **"Stop" is not what they said, and not what your ending does.**
   * People inside the race have said: this is dangerous; coordinate; regulate us; we would go slower if everyone did.
   * Almost nobody inside asked for a stop. The 2023 pause letter came mostly from outside the labs.
   * The film's own accord doesn't stop the fire. It sets the pace: NO FASTER THAN WE CAN SEE.
   * The bible says "would like it to slow down". Somewhere between bible and screen it became "stop".
2. **"Could not" is the labs' own defence, stated as fact.**
   * Much of this audience watched companies ask for regulation in principle and then fight specific bills: the SB 1047 fight in 2024, and industry super-PAC money against state AI laws in 2025.
   * To them this couplet is an alibi. It also gets the film's most solemn beat: a black screen and solo piano.
   * v2 removed v1's heroism ("Slow us down. All of us. Together.") but kept v1's point of view. The racers are still the only people whose inner life the film shows.
3. **The film can't know what "many" wanted.**
   * It can say what they said and what they did. That is the real middle ground between hero and villain.
   * It also serves the producer's "as yet undetermined" lab better than a claim about what that lab truly wants.
   * If the film is ever credited to an AI system, or to anyone at a lab, a line about labs' private wishes will be read as a self-portrait.

**Change the silence couplet.** Three options:
* **Recommended: "Many in the race said it should slow. / None would slow alone."** It reports speech and action. "Would" makes it a choice inside a trap rather than a helplessness. "Slow" matches the ending, and "So someone else lit a beacon" still follows.
* **Closest to the current wording:** "Many in the race wished it would slow. / None of them could slow it alone." Here "slow it" means the race, and that is true: no single runner could.
* **Driest:** "No one inside the race could end it." It's one line, so the silence has more room.

**Also change 820–900.** "Each said: if we stop, the others won't." is accurate, but it only gives the game-theory half: nobody can afford to stop first. The argument racers actually make, and believe, is a moral one: **"Each said: if not us, someone worse."** The most safety-minded lab says it, and so does the most hawkish defence ministry. Giving it to everyone is fair to the lab, exact about the ministry, and passes no verdict on either. Next to "said it should slow", it shows the trap without accusing anyone: the same people say both.

**M2 · A 490–650, C 490–565 · The fire is only ever a prize; the risk half this audience works on is missing**

* **Issue.** Every line treats the fire as something that can be held: "Whoever held it alone would hold the world", NO SINGLE HAND SHALL HOLD IT, the grasp. Nothing in the text admits that we don't understand what we built, or that nobody may be able to hold it. The vortex that dwarfs the towers hints at it, but the text points the other way.
* **Why this room objects.** For the alignment half of the audience, the race is dangerous mainly because it makes care unaffordable and outruns understanding, not only because somebody wins. A film that makes the danger *who gets the fire* has quietly assumed the technical problem away. That is the nuclear-weapons framing, and it's the first thing they'll say.
* **Change.** Add one clause, and let the oaths pay it off.
  * **A 490–550:** "And it began to think." becomes **"And it began to think. No one could see how."** Now NO FASTER THAN WE CAN SEE and NO FORGE IN THE DARK answer something inside the fire, not only rivals hiding from each other. It needs about 15 more frames: run it ≈490–565 and slide "Whoever held it alone…" to ≈580–648.
  * **Or, with more dread** (pick one, not both): after "Whoever held it alone would hold the world", add "If it could be held at all."
  * **C 490–565:** "A fire that could think." becomes **"A fire that could think, with a will of its own."** In Tolkien the Ring's own will is canon. This also gives the Eye a meaning that isn't a nation: the thing they're racing for, looking back.

**M3 · Cut C · 565–635, 1112–1190, 2072–2399, 2512–2600 · Tolkien's lesson is "no hand at all", so the fire and the Ring must be different things**

C's text makes the whole technology the One Ring: "we forged a new power. / A fire that could think. / One ring to rule them all." It then says it could not be unmade, and lays it in the council's hearth under a vow that no *single* hand shall hold it.

**Why Tolkien readers object** (in this audience, that's most people):
1. **The Council of Elrond refuses to use the Ring at all.** It corrupts whoever wields it, the wise and well-meaning included. Gandalf's and Galadriel's refusals are the moral of the book. A council that keeps the Ring in shared custody is Boromir's proposal run by a committee, and "no single hand" reads as "many hands may".
2. **The One Ring has no good use.** So in this cut, vow IV (WHAT IT GIVES, IT GIVES TO ALL) has nothing to give. To anyone who watched *The Rings of Power*, "gifts for all" is Annatar, Lord of Gifts, handing rings to every people. That's the wrong echo for the climax.
3. **It tilts C toward doom.** The technology itself becomes Sauron's instrument.

**Change** (text only; the picture already supports it):
* **At 1112**, change "This one could not be unmade." to **"This fire could not be unmade."** The point that it can't be undone belongs to the technology, which is where it's true.
* **At 2512–2600**, add **"The Ring was unmade in a fire everyone had lit."** This slot is empty in C; A uses it for "It ended the race."
  * Your picture already tells this story. The race forged the fire into a Ring (≈600–622). At the council the Ring lies in a hearth every emissary fed, no hand touches it, the white takes it, and the hearth becomes the sun that falls on everyone.
  * The line turns the eucatastrophe into the actual governance claim: you can't uninvent the fire, but you can melt down the prize of one hand ruling all.
  * It echoes the silence line: "the fire that forged it" becomes "a fire everyone had lit". It swaps a lone quest to the enemy's forge for a fire the whole world fed.
  * Vow IV now refers to the fire, so it stands, and so does NO SINGLE HAND.
* **Keep:**
  * "One Ring to rule them all." It now names the prize, which is right.
  * The inscription kindling in the hearth. It rhymes with Gandalf's test of the Ring in Frodo's fire.
  * NO FORGE IN THE DARK.

**M4 · A, B, C · 880–960 · The world cracks along the first island chain**

* **Issue.** The ember globe is framed on East Asia and the Western Pacific. The plates part along a seam from east of Japan past Taiwan and the Philippines, separating mainland Asia from the Pacific. `scene_c.Globe` seeds its cracks "from several origins (the race is everywhere)", but the camera only ever shows one hemisphere, and it's this one. In A it follows "if we stop, the others won't". In C it arrives under "our enemies will".
* **Why this room objects.** That seam is the most-discussed flashpoint of the AI race: a war over Taiwan, and the chip supply chain. A film that never names a country shouldn't let its camera name one. In C it completes B1. B, which has no words, carries the same image.
* **Change.** Re-aim `Globe.rot()` in `shots/embers/scene_c.py` (currently `a = 2.3 + 0.0045*(t-880)`, with a 20° tilt). Two options:
  * Look down from high northern latitudes. North America, Europe, Russia, China and India then all sit on the rim together, and the seams run *between* them.
  * Or turn the globe far enough over the 80 frames that the fracture crosses every continent.

  Either way, it's about 80 frames per cut.

**M5 · A, B, and C until DAWN_C lands · 2440–2623 · The dawn switches on the lights of "dark Africa"**

* **Issue.** The DAWN camera is over central Africa looking east, and the sun rises beside an unmistakable Horn of Africa. From about 2440, "thousands of village hearths bloom across dark Africa and Arabia, spreading out from the beacons" (globe NOTES). In A, that is the picture under "It ended the race."
* **Why this room objects.**
  * It combines the Black Marble "dark continent" image with the "AI for development" pitch. Africa becomes the place that receives the gift, not a party to the bargain.
  * It contradicts your own montage, where the desert and karst answerers light their own beacons.
  * It's the least two-way picture you could draw of WHAT IT GIVES, IT GIVES TO ALL. Rural Africa isn't short of hearths.
* **Change** (pick one):
  * Keep the sunrise over the Horn, because dawn over humanity's oldest home is a beautiful image, but drop the lights blooming into the dark. The golden web, already on screen, can carry "shared" by itself.
  * Or make the bloom global and simultaneous. Every existing light on the night side warms to the web's gold at once, rich cities included. It reads as shared, not delivered.
  * Or give A and B the mountain sunrise being built for C, without the eagles. The sun rising on the ranges where the first beacon was lit closes the loop. It also avoids the look of a keynote slide (m9).

### MINOR

**m1 · Cut A · 4–82 · The opening frame line can't be read, and its arithmetic breaks the red-scarf reveal**

* **Too fast.** "A story they might tell, a hundred years from now, / about the years we are living through." is 17 words. With the letter stagger and fades, both lines are fully visible together for under a second and readable for about 2.5 s. It needs about 5 s.
* **The ages don't add up.** The elder lit the first beacon as a young woman, in "the years we are living through". If she is telling the story "a hundred years from now", she is about 120; the bible says she lit it "~60 years earlier".
* **Why it matters.** This line is the film's main honesty device, the "might", and this audience will do the scarf arithmetic.
* **Change.** Replace it with **"A story they might tell of us, a lifetime from now."**
  * It's 11 words and keeps "might", the future frame and the mapping.
  * It makes the heroine one of the audience's own generation, so "Who lit the first one?" lands on the viewer.
  * If the picture fade-in allows, hold it until about 90–95.

**m2 · Cut A · 2512–2600 · "It ended the race." is the only flatly certain claim in a film framed as "might"**

Races ended by treaty end over years of talks, not overnight, and your title already says so. Change it to **"In time, it ended the race."**

**m3 · Cut A · 215, 660, 1370 · "So… So… So…"**

Three sentences starting with "So" is the rhythm of a debate argument, not a legend. Keep the one that matters ("So someone else lit a beacon."). At 660, drop it: **"The companies and the countries raced."**

**m4 · Cut C · 660–730 · "And every kingdom wanted it." drops the companies**

C's race becomes purely a contest between states: the Manhattan Project framing. That loses the producers' own point that money drives the race as much as fear does. Change it to **"And every kingdom and every guild wanted it."**

**m5 · Cut C · 565–635 · "One ring" should be "One Ring"**

The verse capitalises it, and C's own silence line says "the Ring". (A nit in the bible: the line is six words, not five.)

**m6 · Cut C · DAWN_C (not yet rendered) · The eagles**

This audience decodes the allegory frame by frame. Great birds arriving out of the sky at the moment of rescue will make them ask "what are the eagles?", and both obvious answers are bad:
* a deus ex machina (the "why didn't they fly the eagles to Mordor" meme);
* benevolent superintelligence arriving to save us, the most techno-utopian reading there is.

Keep them small, late, and flying away into the light, never on a musical hit. Or cut them.

**m7 · A, C · 2072–2399 · Who is at the table?**

"None could stop alone" only pays off if the racers themselves are bound together. The emissaries read as peoples or nations. That's fine, since states bind their companies, but nothing ties the emissaries to the eight towers. If ACCORD is ever re-rendered for another reason, echo the tower silhouettes in the emissaries' staffs or in their shadows radiating from the hearth. Then the viewer sees that the racers themselves came to the table. It isn't worth a re-render on its own.

**m8 · A, B, C · 1920–2087 · The answer reaches the Americas last**

The globe lights Asia first and only later leaps over the horizon. C's map runs from the Himalaya to Asia, then Africa and Europe, and reaches the Americas at about 2040. That's defensible, even apt: the countries with the most to win answer last. But this audience will read it as a comment on the US. Make sure it's meant.

**m9 · A, B, C · 1920–2087 and 2400–2623 · Tech-marketing imagery at the climax**

* The golden web over the night Earth, and C's map at its widest, read as a network diagram, the house style of telecom and AI advertising ("connecting the world").
* The sun rising over the curve of the Earth is every keynote's closing slide.

In a film about restraining the builders, that's their imagery at the climax. For the web, use fewer, longer chains that follow ridgelines and line of sight: fire, not fibre. For the dawn, see M5.

**m10 · Cut A · 340–440 · Optional: "We taught a new kind of fire…"**

"We" is defensible (the species), but it blurs who did the teaching and whose words they were, which this audience actively argues about. **"They fed a new kind of fire everything we had ever written."** is truer. It also makes vow IV a debt repaid rather than charity: it was made of everyone's words, so it gives to all.

*Out of scope, one line:* Tolkien's rights holders police the names and imagery closely. Get a clearance check on "One Ring to rule them all" and the slit-pupil Eye before C is shown publicly.

## Top 5

1. **C: stop taking a side (B1).** Replace "the free peoples" with "And hill by hill, the world answered." Move "our enemies will" off the Eye, and re-aim the globe.
2. **A: give the builders' words, not their defence (M1).** Change the lines to "Each said: if not us, someone worse." and "Many in the race said it should slow. / None would slow alone." This reports what they said and did, and swaps "stop" for "slow" and "could" for "would".
3. **A and C: admit nobody can see inside the fire (M2).** In A, "And it began to think. No one could see how." In C, "A fire that could think, with a will of its own." This brings in the alignment half of the risk, and the oaths then answer it.
4. **C: separate the fire from the Ring (M3).** Change the silence line to "This fire could not be unmade." and add "The Ring was unmade in a fire everyone had lit." This keeps the best line in the film, makes it true to Tolkien, and turns the eucatastrophe into the real governance claim.
5. **All cuts: don't let the camera assign geography (M4 + M5).** Crack the globe between all the powers, not along the first island chain. Drop, globalise or replace the dawn that lights up "dark Africa".

Best of the minors: **"A story they might tell of us, a lifetime from now."** (m1). It's readable, it fixes the 120-year-old heroine, and it makes "Who lit the first one?" land on the viewer.
