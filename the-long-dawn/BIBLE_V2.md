# THE LONG DAWN — v2 production bible (read this before BIBLE.md)

v1 is finished and delivered (`out/the_long_dawn.mp4`). v2 makes **three committed cuts** of the
film from one shared picture and (mostly) shared score, fixes what the producer's review found, and adds a new,
more ambitious 3-D shot. BIBLE.md still holds the world, the look, the characters and the
original shot briefs; where the two disagree, **this file wins**.

## 1. What was wrong with v1 (the producer's notes, and the director's diagnosis)

* **It never decided what it was.** Half Tolkien-homage (beacons, a council, a ring of power,
  eucatastrophe), half not (orbital views, city rooftops). Half precise about the real world (the
  race logic, "its makers said slow us down", policy oaths), half generic myth. Each half dilutes
  the other.
* **The labs were cast as the heroes** ("Then even its makers said: Slow us down. All of us.
  Together." is the moral turning point). That's wrong. The builders are neither heroes nor
  villains: they are runners carried by the race's own gradient — competition, capital, the fear
  that someone worse gets there first. Many of them would like it to slow down; none can stop
  alone. The turn has to come from outside the race.
* **Borderline cringe — a death sentence for this kind of film.** Almost all of it sits in the
  second half: the trailer-voice triplet "Rivals. Strangers. Enemies. / They answered anyway.",
  the PSA preamble "They agreed on little — but they agreed on this:", "together" in 27 languages
  (a UN poster), "It did not end our differences.", the end card "The Kindling is now.", pastel
  toy-like emissaries at the table, a clay-mannequin heroine in close-up.
* **The music's second half changes tone.** The first half is modern, dark and restrained (glassy
  FM "thinking" arpeggios, taiko race, dark strings, a lone piano). From the first beacon on it
  becomes a traditional triumphal hymn: the theme handed round the orchestra like a showcase,
  synthesized "aah" choir, organ, glockenspiel/celesta sparkle, harp glissando sweeps, a bell hit
  per oath (Mickey-mousing), cymbal-and-gong victory at the dawn. It reads as uplift-by-numbers.

## 2. Three cuts, one film

All cuts share every frame and the first half of the score except where noted.

### Cut A — THE ALLEGORY (explicit)
The mapping to our moment is made unmistakable and precise — **without ever saying "AI"**. It
opens by stating its frame: a legend told a century from now about the years we are living
through. Every beat maps to something true (the fire read everything we ever wrote; companies and
countries raced; the racers could not stop alone; someone *else* lit the first beacon; the world
answered; the council agreed on hard, specific things; the race ended). The Tolkien shape is kept
because a legend is the right form for it — but nothing is quoted, and nothing is winked at.

### Cut B — THE LEGEND (for its own sake)
No allegory to serve, no policy to encode. A wordless myth of fire and people: an old woman and a
child at a festival of fires; the vision in the flame (a fire born from writing, a hunger, a
reaching hand, a world cracking, a single ember dying); a young woman on a frozen summit striking
flint in the dark; a chain of beacons racing across the ranges; people everywhere lighting fires;
a planet laced with light; a council whose torches become one fire; the sun; the child lighting
the beacon. **The only text is the title.** Every choice is made for beauty and feeling. Because
there is no text, nothing is dimmed for legibility: renders that calmed a "text band" are redone
without it for this cut.

### Cut C — THE TOLKIEN CUT (explicitly Lord of the Rings)
The producers also want one cut that wears its Tolkien heart openly — not the general archetype,
but recognisably *The Lord of the Rings*: the Ring, the Eye, the beacons, the map, the Council, the
dawn in the east, the eagles. The allegory still maps to our moment, and it earns its sharpest
line from the one place the old story and ours differ: Tolkien's Ring could be unmade in the fire
that forged it; this power cannot be un-invented, so it must never be held by a single hand.
Everything is ORIGINAL work in homage — our own designs, an invented elvish-looking script (no real
Tolkien text on the Ring), no film dialogue, no borrowed melodies, no names of Middle-earth places
or people, no logos. The one quotation is the famous five-word line below.

Picture changes (C only):
* **The Ring.** The crown of fire becomes a plain gold band, burning with an inscription in an
  invented flowing script; it forms out of the thinking fire (KINDLING) and floats above the towers.
* **The Eye.** The RACE's vortex resolves into a lidless Eye of fire, slit pupil and all, before
  the cut to the ember globe.
* **The grasp.** The great hand closes on the Ring.
* **The map.** THE WORLD ANSWERS becomes our Earth drawn as a Tolkien-style map on parchment (ink
  coastlines, hatched hills, little mountain glyphs, a compass rose, no place names), with fire and
  threads of flame racing out from the high mountains across it (new MAP shot, same frames).
* **The Council.** The Ring lies in the fire at the centre of the stone table; the four vows are
  carved as in cut A.
* **Dawn in the east.** Instead of the sunrise over Earth from orbit: the sun breaks over the
  eastern ranges of the Blender mountain world, light flooding the valleys and cloud sea, and a
  flight of great eagles crosses the light (new DAWN_C shot, same frames).

Music (C only): the second half gets its own score in the epic-fantasy tradition — horn calls
passed from peak to peak like signal fires, strings, timpani, a choral colour used sparingly and
never cheesy — with ORIGINAL melodies (the film's own Beacon theme); nothing borrowed from any
existing film score.

### Tone rules for all cuts (the cringe blacklist)
Restraint, understatement, awe, dread, specificity. Show; don't tell. The references are *Arrival*,
*Interstellar*, *1917*'s flare-lit night, *Blade Runner 2049*'s scale, the *Tale of the Three
Brothers* shadow-play, the LOTR beacon run — for feeling, never for quotation. **Never:** slogans
presented as profound, unity-poster imagery, multilingual "together", trailer-voice triplets,
speeches, heroic tech-makers, cute sparkle, uplift-by-numbers, triumphal victory music,
fake-sounding choirs, Mickey-mousing a hit onto every word, characters that look like clay dolls
in close-up. Fire is always the protagonist and the only saturated warm light.

## 4. Departments (v2)

Output folders use a layered lookup in the edit: `renders/<dept>_<A|B|C>/` → `renders/<dept>_v2/` →
`renders/<dept>/`, so a department only writes the frames it changes.

* **MUSIC** — on the v2 timeline, re-score from the FIRST BEACON (v2 1200) to the end: score **AB**
  in the first half's sound world (§1) for cuts A and B, and score **C** (epic-fantasy tradition)
  for cut C; the first half (0–1199) is shared and kept. Rebuild the SFX list for the new timeline
  (incl. the Beacon Run). Stems + masters → `music/out/v2/` (`final_AB.wav`, `final_C.wav`).
* **ACCORD** — dark cloaked emissaries (no pastel toys); the "together" ring replaced by a carved
  ornament band that ignites in a sweep; no T12 dimming. Variant A: the four oaths, crisp and
  legible. Variant B: no words (ornament only; the roll becomes one slow continuous turn). Variant
  C: as A, with the Ring lying in the hearth fire. → `renders/accord_A|B|C/` (src 1912–2247).
* **MOUNTAIN (Blender, GPU)** — builds one night-mountain world in Blender and renders THE BEACON
  RUN (`renders/run/`, v2 1520–1679, all cuts) and DAWN_C (`renders/dawn_C/`, v2 2400–2655, cut C).
* **HILLS** — the heroine of the FIRST BEACON close-ups (1236–1375) no longer reads as a clay doll
  → `renders/hills_v2/` (src numbering), all cuts.
* **EMBERS** — towers that read as towers made of embers (not CAD wireframes) and the vortex's
  edge-on moment fixed (all cuts) → `renders/embers_v2/`; cut B without text-band attenuation →
  `renders/embers_B/`; cut C's Ring, Eye and grasp-on-the-Ring → `renders/embers_C/` (src numbering).
* **MAP** — cut C's THE WORLD ANSWERS as a Tolkien-style map of our Earth → `renders/map_C/`
  (v2 numbering 1920–2087).
* **GLOBE** — cut B DAWN without the text-band calm → `renders/globe_B/`.

## 5. Conventions (unchanged from v1 unless stated)
1920×804, linear HDR → `lib/look.py: finish()` → `save_png()`; no grain, letterbox or text in
delivered frames. Deterministic frames. On this Mac: `source ~/.venvs/longdawn/env.sh` first; at
most 2 render processes per department; RAM < 2.5 GB each; don't run git commands that change the
repo; don't touch other departments' folders; back up anything you overwrite.
