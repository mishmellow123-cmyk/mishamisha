# THE LONG DAWN

*A short film, just under two minutes long, made entirely in code.*

> *Why do we light the fires?*
> *To remember the night the whole world answered.*

**Watch:** [`out/the_long_dawn.mp4`](out/the_long_dawn.mp4) (1080p, 2.39:1, stereo)

On a hill in a good future, an old woman and a child watch beacon fires lit on
every hill around them. The child asks why. The old woman tells the legend of
the **Kindling**: the age when we made a fire that could think. Every kingdom
raced to hold it alone, and the race nearly consumed the world. Then the fire's
own makers asked to be slowed, all of them together, and someone on a cold
mountain lit a beacon. Then another was lit, and another, across deserts, ice,
jungle, cities and sea, until rivals and enemies stood around one stone table
and swore four oaths:

    NO SINGLE HAND SHALL HOLD IT
    NO FASTER THAN WE CAN SEE
    NO FORGE IN THE DARK
    WHAT IT GIVES, IT GIVES TO ALL

*It did not end our differences. It ended the race.*

## What it's about

The shape of the story is the one Tolkien gave us: separated peoples who
distrust each other unite against a shadow, the beacons are lit and answered,
the Ring is refused instead of wielded, and joy turns suddenly at dawn (his
word for it was *eucatastrophe*). The film tells that story about the arrival
of powerful AI. It never says "AI". The AI is *a new kind of fire, a fire that
could think*.

Each beat maps onto something real. Placed in that mythic frame, the real
parts hit harder than an explainer would:

| in the legend | in our world |
|---|---|
| the fire is kindled from glyphs of every script, every equation and song | models trained on everything humanity ever wrote |
| *Whoever held it alone would hold the world* | concentration of power |
| *So the kingdoms raced. Each said: if we stop, they win.* | the arms-race trap, and the risk of war |
| *Then even its makers said: Slow us down. All of us. Together.* | leading labs asking for coordinated limits |
| someone lights a beacon; ordinary people answer | civil society and ordinary people moving first |
| rivals, strangers, enemies around one table | international agreement across political differences |
| **No single hand shall hold it** | no monopoly on the frontier, checks and balances |
| **No faster than we can see** | pacing the frontier to what can be understood and verified |
| **No forge in the dark** | verification: no secret training runs, compute in the open |
| **What it gives, it gives to all** | shared benefits, which also removes the reason to race |
| *It did not end our differences. It ended the race.* | coordination without uniformity |
| sunrise over Earth; lights bloom where the night map was dark | a flourishing future that reaches everyone |

A few smaller choices:

* **The fire's colour tells the story.** Ordinary fire is orange. The thinking
  fire is born white and ice-blue. During the race it bleeds to crimson. Once
  shared, it turns gold.
* **The music's "call"** (a rising fifth and fourth, like a horn across
  mountains) is the beacon. In the race it is corrupted into a tritone alarm.
  Each beacon in the montage answers it in a new instrument. At dawn it comes
  back whole, in the major key on the same tonic: the same world, in new light.
* **The red scarf.** The young woman who lights the first beacon on the cold
  mountain wears a long red scarf. So does the old woman on the hill. When the
  child asks *who lit the first one?* she doesn't answer. She hands over her
  torch.
* **The sky of the future** has a faint orbital ring, and the dark side of the
  Moon is scattered with city lights. The film never explains either.

## How it was made

Everything on screen and in the soundtrack was generated by code in this
folder. No cameras, no stock footage, no image or video models, no sampled
audio beyond a CC0 orchestral library. The work ran on a 4-core CPU with no GPU.
It was split into departments that worked in parallel, the way a small studio
would:

| department | frames | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| `shots/hills` | 0–359, 1200–1439, 2460–2807 | the old woman, the child, and the first beacon: 2.5D parallax landscapes, SDF-puppet silhouettes, verlet-simulated scarf, procedural HDR fire |
| `shots/embers` | 300–1199 | the legend seen in the fire: millions of 3D particles, depth of field, motion blur |
| `shots/montage` | 1440–1767 | six beacon ignitions around the world, cut on the beat |
| `shots/globe` | 1752–1935, 2232–2495 | Earth at night with a spreading web of fire, and the sunrise over the limb |
| `shots/accord` | 1912–2247 | the stone table, the shadows, the oaths, *together* in 27 languages |
| `music/` | 0–117 s | original score (72 BPM, D minor to D major) and sound design |
| `edit/` | all | cut, dissolves, titles, grade, grain, letterbox, mix, master |

The production bible every department worked from is [`BIBLE.md`](BIBLE.md),
and the music brief is [`music/CUES.md`](music/CUES.md). Picture and sound are
locked to one grid: 24 fps and 72 BPM, so one beat is exactly 20 frames.

### Open material used

* Natural Earth vector data (public domain): coastlines, land, populated places
* Earth texture maps from the three.js examples (derived from NASA Blue/Black Marble, public domain)
* VSCO-2 Community Edition orchestral samples (CC0)
* Fonts: Cinzel, Cormorant Garamond, EB Garamond (SIL OFL); Noto (SIL OFL)

### Finishing pass

The departments rendered in a 4-core cloud container and handed their frames over as clips
(`handoff/`); the film was finished in a second session on a laptop, which also fixed the
weakest moments:

* **GRASP** (960–1039): the ember hand rebuilt as one blended hand of cracked, burning crust
  that turns and closes into a fist (`shots/embers/scene_c.py`, `timeline.py`).
* **FIRST BEACON** reveal (1360–1439): a true 3-D range of moonlit peaks instead of ridge
  cards (`shots/hills/peaks.py`, switched by `TRUE_PEAKS` in `beacon.py`).
* **ICE** (1580–1639): the aurora rebuilt as three alias-free curtains over a starry sky,
  with a moonlit massif and a true mirror in the fjord (`shots/montage/s3_ice.py`).
* **SEA** (1720–1767): the bow flare made the hero light of the shot; the coast beacons
  re-spaced so the chain reads left to right into the globe (`shots/montage/s6_sea.py`).
* **Sound** (`edit/mix.py`): restores the score's designed breaths (before 640, 1040 and
  2240), reinforces the RACE entrance and the DAWN arrival with the score's own instruments,
  lifts two ignitions the music masked, and masters to −16 LUFS / −1.2 dBTP.
* **Titles**: story lines at 56 px for phone legibility; the last line, *The Kindling is
  now.*, stands alone on black after the final chord, so the film runs 119 s.

### Re-render

```bash
# each department: see shots/<dept>/NOTES.md and music/NOTES.md
# (on macOS point LONGDAWN_SYSFONTS at a folder with truetype/noto + opentype/noto)
python3 edit/mix.py                                          # -> music/out/final_mix.wav
python3 edit/assemble.py --audio music/out/final_mix.wav     # -> out/the_long_dawn.mp4
```
