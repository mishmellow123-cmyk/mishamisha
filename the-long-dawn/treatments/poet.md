# THE LONG DAWN v3: three films from one night

*Treatment by the poet-filmmaker, writers' room, 26 Sep 2026. Image first, few words.*

## What went wrong in draft 1, in one paragraph

In draft 1 one element had three jobs. Fire was the danger (the thinking fire), the alarm (the beacons) and the reward (the gold at the table and the dawn). Those jobs pull against each other, so the eye could never tell which fire was which. That is why the metaphor felt incoherent, and why the words had to do the explaining. Words doing the explaining are what made it feel on the nose. A and C then told that one blurred story twice, at a run, in two minutes. The pictures were never the problem. The problem was that the fire meant too many things at once.

## The rule for v3

**Each cut gives fire one meaning and keeps it from its first frame to its last.** The three cuts share one world: the hill, the cold mountain, the ranges, the stone table and the red scarf. But each tells a different story, in a different visual language, with a different ending.

| | **A: THE WATCH** (allegory) | **B: THE STOLEN SUN** (wordless legend) | **C: THE REFUSAL** (Tolkien cut) |
|---|---|---|---|
| what fire means | sight: a light carried at the pace we can see by | the sun: light that must be called back | power: the One Ring forged from the gift |
| story shape | an argument: a trap, a turn, a crossing | a rite: a theft, a vigil, a return, and the rite repeated | a quest turned inside out: a temptation, a refusal, a council |
| the turn | someone outside the race lights a fire that forges nothing and lets everyone see | a spark from the dying sun becomes the first fire in human hands | the Ring falls to someone who will not hold it |
| visual language | night photography, two speeds of camera | shadow-play, cut paper, symmetry, sideways motion | an illuminated chronicle: parchment, ink, gold leaf, sweeping aerials |
| signature new sequence | THE SPRINT, FORGES IN THE LIGHT, THE THAW | THE ROAD EAST (a paper-theatre one-take) | THE RING FALLS, THE RING IN THE SNOW, THE LAND BECOMES THE MAP |
| ending | dusk; the question; the torch handed on | dawn on the hill; the red scarf around the sleeping child | a scar in an old palm; no words |
| runtime | 5:26 | 4:06 | 5:50 |

**The producers' core message, felt rather than said.** The villain is never the fire. The fire is the gift, and none of the three films tries to put it out. The villain is the race: everyone running into the dark with a light they cannot yet hold, and every step toward the edge paying. The answer in all three is the oldest way people ever moved fire across a land. It went from height to height, and each watcher lit their own only when they could *see* the last one. A beacon chain cannot go faster than sight. It works in the open, it needs many hands, and nobody can run ahead alone. The title means the same thing: a dawn that is long because it is deliberate. That thought is in all three films, but no character or caption ever says it.

# CUT A: THE WATCH (the allegory)

### (1) Logline
On a festival night a lifetime from now, an old woman keeps a question unanswered. Her story: every forge once raced to hold alone a fire that could think, and every step toward the edge paid. Then someone outside the race lit a fire that forged nothing and only let people see. Height by height, others lit theirs, and in that light the racers could see each other and let the fire be carried at the pace of sight, toward a dawn that belonged to everyone.

### (2) The central idea, and why it holds from first frame to last
**Fire as sight.** In A the danger is never the fire. It is fire *in the dark*, carried by runners who cannot see the ground, or each other. Every image is on one axis, blind or seeing:

**Why the turn follows inevitably.** The trap is made of darkness. "If not us, someone worse" only makes sense when you cannot see what the others are doing. So the only thing that can undo the trap is not a stronger forge. It is light that takes no side. That is the beacon: it makes nothing, it wins nothing, it lets people see. Once lit, it spreads the only way a beacon can: each watcher lights when they see the last, never faster. The film's answer, *pace, not prohibition*, is simply how a beacon chain behaves. Nobody has to argue for it. And because seeing is the answer, the two forges that raced hardest open to each other first; that is not a treaty, it is two people finally able to see. The rest follows.

**Look and motion (A's own film).** Night photography: the flare-lit dark of *1917*, *Arrival*'s restraint. Blue-black, three fire colours with fixed meanings (ice-white for the thinking fire, crimson for the race, orange for human hands). Gold is the race's seduction until the council; after it, gold is only dawn. **A has two camera speeds, and the switch is the film's argument.** Before the turn the camera sprints: fast pushes, whip pans, strobing cuts on every beat. After the turn it never moves faster than a person walks: long lenses, slow cranes, and every cut motivated by an eyeline, *someone sees a fire, then we cut to it*. The edit itself becomes a beacon chain.

### (3) Runtime: 5:26, and why
Draft 1 was rushed because it tried to argue its case, turn and resolve all in 124 s. A needs three things that take time. First, silence at its centre: 20 s of black after the grasp. Second, a relay slow enough to *feel* slow: the pace is the message, so it cannot be cut on the beat as a montage. Third, a dawn long enough to be a dawn and not a flash. Any longer and the argument would sag. At 5:26 the first act runs just over two minutes, the dark twenty seconds and the relay two minutes, and the last minute belongs to light.

### (8) Why A is neither forced nor on the nose
* **One lexicon.** Every word comes from the fire's own world: *fire, forge, hold, dark, light, see, watch, gold*. None comes from the technology's world. The mapping is unmistakable because of three plain facts: everything we wrote, a mind no one can see into, and a race that pays. It is never because of a term. No policy word, no institution, no country, no mechanism.
* **The metaphor is explained once, late, after the picture has already shown it.** "And in its light, each saw the others" arrives as the shutters open. It confirms what the viewer has just understood, which is the opposite of a caption telling them what to feel.
* **The north star is felt, not recognized.** The work comes out of the dark: the shutters. Many hands move together: many first fires, and the torches carried outward. Pace comes before power: a relay can't outrun sight, and the camera slows. The two largest open to each other first. The pressure comes from ordinary people on the hills, not from the forges. Every one of those is a physical behaviour of fire and people at night. Not one is a picture of a real institution.
* **The dread is honest.** The race is beautiful and it pays; that is why it is terrifying. The globe goes to ash and *stays* ash for a second, and the black after the grasp is the longest silence in the film. The hope is earned: the dawn arrives only after the longest, slowest stretch of the film, and nobody wins it.
* **The balance stays even.** Draft 1 was literal in its words and mythic in its pictures. A is mythic in both, and grounded in its logic: the trap and its undoing are exactly true of our moment.

# CUT B: THE STOLEN SUN (the wordless legend)

### (1) Logline
On the longest night of the year, a grandmother and a child light the festival fire, and in its sparks the old legend plays. A hand of Night once seized the sun, and its last spark fell to a young woman on the highest peak. The peoples of the world passed her fire east, height by height, until their gathered fires rekindled the dawn. And so, every year, they keep the fires until morning.

### (2) The central idea, and why it holds from first frame to last
**Fire as the sun's own light, held in human hands.** B is a solstice myth, the kind every culture tells: the Yalda vigil, Yule, Dongzhi, Amaterasu drawn out of her cave by a festival. It serves no allegory; it is coherent by the logic of ritual. If the sun is fire and it has been taken, only fire can call it back. Enough fire, carried together, becomes the sun again.

| image | what it means |
|---|---|
| the child lights the festival fire at dusk | the rite begins (B opens where A ends) |
| sparks rise and slow into the stars | fire is the stuff of the sky |
| the sun asleep in the dark: a calm gold fire among the stars (the thinking fire in gold) | the world's light, resting |
| old kingdoms rise around it, lit by it; it lifts as a crown | the world as it was, living by that light |
| Night: a storm of cold blue-black embers winding round the crown | the long night rising |
| a coal-black hand rises and closes on the sun; white; black | one hand wanted the light for itself, and the world went dark |
| the last spark of the sun drifts down, dims, and as it dies a flint spark strikes in the dark | the sun's last fire passes into human hands |
| the young woman, in silhouette, lights the highest beacon | the nearest fire to the sky |
| THE ROAD EAST: one sideways journey through cut-paper worlds; at every height someone lights a fire as the last comes into view; the sky behind warms from black to indigo to rose | fire walked, not run, toward where the sun must rise |
| the gathering: every fire brought to one round floor; the ring becomes one fire; it flares white | together, the people's fire becomes a sun |
| out of the white, the sun lifts over the ranges; smoke rising from every summit | the sun answered |
| the hill at dawn: the fires burned out; the child asleep in the red scarf; the sun touches them; birdsong | the vigil kept, the story passed on: the scarf, not a torch |

**Why the turn follows inevitably.** The match cut makes it causal, not symbolic. The sun's dying spark and the woman's first spark are one light, so the story's hope literally lives in her hands. Once fire is the sun's light, the road east is the only road. The gathering is the only way to make enough of it, and the dawn is the only possible answer. The frame closes the circle: the grandmother kept the vigil as the young woman once did, and now the scarf is around the child.

**Look and motion (B's own film).** Shadow-play. Everything human is silhouette against light that glows *through* a surface: paper, smoke, snow, sky. It is *The Tale of the Three Brothers*, Lotte Reiniger and a Balinese wayang screen, but lit like cinema: fire shows through translucent layers, and haze sits between them. Compositions are symmetrical and centred, like painted panels. Motion is lateral (the scroll, the frieze, the procession), never a push into depth. No faces are ever lit; no close-ups. The embers act gets a B palette of gold and cold blue with no crimson; this legend has no race. The shared renders are regraded through a shadow-play pass (silhouette extraction, paper grain, backlight halation). The one new signature, THE ROAD EAST, is built in that language from the start.

### (3) Runtime: 4:06, and why
A legend is a song. It should be told in one breath and remembered whole. B carries no argument, so it needs no room for argument. What it needs is a long middle journey (the road east, 44 s), because in a wordless film distance is the only way to make the effort felt. It also needs a slow return to the hill at dawn, because the payoff is a sunrise the child actually sees. Longer, and a wordless film would start asking for words.

None. The title only.

### (8) Why B is neither forced nor on the nose
* **It is a real kind of myth, not an allegory in disguise.** Solstice fires, the stolen sun and a vigil until dawn are older than any reading we could put on them. Nobody watching B is asked to decode anything. Viewers who have seen A will feel the rhyme between one hand seizing the light and many hands bringing it back, and B never points at it.
* **It is B's own plot, not A without subtitles.** There is no race, no forge, no council, no oath and no question. There is a theft, a spark that changes hands, a procession, a gathering and a morning. It opens at dusk with the fire lit and ends at dawn with the fire out; A begins and ends at dusk, with fires being lit.
* **The dread is real in its own terms.** The hand closes and the world goes black; the spark nearly dies in the dark, and all the hope in the film is held in one pair of hands for six seconds. The hope is earned by distance: forty-four seconds of fires carried one by one.
* **Its beauty is for its own sake.** Every choice in B is made for a single standard: would a child remember this picture for the rest of their life? That standard rules out anything clever.

# CUT C: THE REFUSAL (the Tolkien cut)

### (1) Logline
The race of every kingdom and every forge beats a fire that could think into a Ring of gold. When the grasping hand closes, the Ring slips it and falls, like a star, into the snow at the feet of a young beacon-keeper on a cold mountain. She will not take it up. She lights her beacon instead, the peoples answer hill by hill, and the Ring is unmade, as the old story demands, in the fire that forged it: a fire that everyone lit.

### (2) The central idea, and why it holds from first frame to last
**Fire as power: the gift, and the Ring forged from it.** C keeps Tolkien's grammar and argues with it at the one place our story differs. In the old story the Ring was unmade in the fire that forged it. Here the fire itself, the new power, cannot be unmade, and nobody tries to. But the Ring is not the fire. The Ring is what *the race* made of the fire: the power of one hand over all, gold beaten out of light. Its forge was every kingdom's fire at once. So by the old story's own rule, the only fire that can unmake it is the fire everyone lights. The ending isn't imposed; it is Tolkien's law, applied honestly.

| image | what it means |
|---|---|
| the thinking fire, born from every word ever written | the gift: a new power, and a will of its own |
| the forges hammer it into a gold band; the inscription burns in, in an invented script | the race turns light into gold and a gift into dominion |
| the Eye opening in the storm | the Ring's will, looking back at whoever wants it |
| the hand closes on the Ring; white; black | one hand's grasp: the closest call |
| the Ring falls out of the dark, a gold star over the moonlit range | the Ring abandons one bearer to look for another (in the book it *left* Gollum: a Ring of Power looks after itself) |
| the melted hole in the snow; the Ring's light on a young face; a hand that hovers and withdraws | the temptation, and the refusal, in silence |
| she drops the Ring into her kindling with the tip of her steel and lights the beacon | she will not carry it alone, and she calls everyone |
| the Beacon Run; the land flattening into an inked map; fire running out along the ranges and rivers | the call goes out, and the old genre's own map answers |
| the council: the Ring small in a cold hearth; every emissary's torch reaches in; the vows carve themselves | a fire everyone lit |
| the Ring slumps and runs into the ash | the power of one hand unmade; the fire remains, shared |
| the sun over the eastern ranges; great birds far off, gliding *away* into the light | eucatastrophe, and the old story's grace note (no rescue from above) |
| an old palm opened to the fire, a pale ring-shaped scar in it; a child's mitten covering it | the cost of having held it for a moment; the wound that never quite heals |

**Why the turn follows inevitably.** Everything in the first act rhymes with the book: the forging, the Eye, the lust of kingdoms. So the audience already knows the Ring's nature, and it knows one law: the Ring goes looking for a new hand. When it falls to a young woman on a mountain, the only question left is the book's own question: will she take it? Tolkien's answer is refusal, but in the book a small company still carries the Ring to the one fire that made it. Our twist is that its forge was everyone's race, so she cannot carry it anywhere alone. She can only light a fire that brings everyone to it. The refusal leads to the beacon, the beacon to the council, and the council to the unmaking, each step forced by the one before.

**Look and motion (C's own film).** An illuminated chronicle. The palette is parchment warmth, iron-gall ink, gold leaf; blacks that are warm, not blue. Transitions pass through paper: ink bleeds into black; a parchment edge scorches and burns through to the next shot. The camera grammar is the epic's: sweeping aerial cranes over ranges, heroic low angles on beacons, a cut on every horn call. The first act keeps the embers' pictures but graded gold and brown; the Eye is the one saturated red in the film. Nothing in C is shot the way A shoots it. There are no long-lens surveillance holds and no strobing; C is broad, slow and grand.

### (3) Runtime: 5:50, and why
C is the epic, and its genre's virtue is patience. It is also the only cut with a protagonist who has to *choose*. A choice needs time on screen, and so does the silence after it. C is built in three books: THE FORGING (84 s), THE COLD MOUNTAIN (60 s) and THE BEACONS to the dawn (130 s), with a prologue and an epilogue. The temptation alone takes sixteen seconds, and those are the sixteen most important seconds in the cut. Anything shorter would reduce the refusal to a gesture.

### (8) Why C is neither forced nor on the nose
* **The homage is structural, not quoted.** The Ring, the Eye, a bearer who is not a king, the beacons, the map, the council, the eucatastrophe and the wound that never quite heals are Tolkien's shapes. Every design, word and note is original. It is recognisably *The Lord of the Rings* because it thinks like it, not because it cites it.
* **It argues with the book at exactly one point**, and that point is the allegory. The power cannot be unmade. The Ring (the prize of one hand, the race's gold) can be. The race forged it, so the whole world's fire unmakes it. Tolkien readers get a real idea, not a costume: the Council's refusal completed by everyone, not by a committee holding it.
* **No lone hero.** She refuses, but she does not save the world. She lights a beacon: *I can't carry this; come.* The unmaking needs every hand, and the film never shows her at the council. The ending's scar is the only sign of what it cost her.
* **The north star, felt.** The Ring's will is haste, secrecy and one hand. The answer is a signal fire everyone can see, many peoples arriving, and a fire everyone lit. Nothing is named, and the only laws in the film are the carved vows.
* **The stakes are Tolkien-sized.** The Eye, the grasp, and a temptation we watch nearly happen: the vision on the band shows her the world at her feet. The dawn is earned by a refusal in silence, not by a victory.
* **The eagles are kept honest.** They are far away, flying away from the camera, after the unmaking, and never on a musical accent. They are a grace note from the old story, not a rescue.

# (7) Music plan (shared)

**What all three share.** There is one melody in the film, the Beacon theme: its **call** (D–A–D, a rising fifth and fourth) and its falling **answer**. Each cut transforms it differently. The rest is shared too:
* one engine and one library (`music/src` v2, VSCO-2 CE, no choir);
* the 72 BPM grid, with a separate timeline for each cut;
* the v2 rules: the SFX own every fire, nothing hits a word or an oath, breaths only where designed, and no victory anywhere;
* stems reused wherever the picture is shared: the KINDLING glass, the race drums, the IMPACT and the silence piano, each re-cut to its cut's timeline.

**A: the pace, made audible.** The first half is v2's (glass, taiko race, IMPACT, piano) with two additions:
* **The gilt:** on every surge of the race, a bright metallic shimmer in the major, sweet over the minor. It is the sound of reward, and the reason nobody stops.
* **THE VERTIGO:** the glass arpeggio splits into two keys at once.

From the roar of the first beacon, the **watch tone** (a low D in the cellos) sounds continuously to the last chord. Over it the music **decelerates by note value** while the harmony opens: 16ths in the race, quarters in the Run, halves in the montage, the council's cycle, and held chords at the dawn with no pulse at all. The film literally slows to the pace of sight. There is one musical resolution in the whole cut: at FORGES IN THE LIGHT, the race's corrupted call (the tritone) heals into the perfect fifth. The dawn is a bloom in D add9 with no tune, and the call enters once, in the cellos, at 292 s. The coda gives the piano the question and strings the answering fires, one family only. The race stays the loudest moment in A; the dawn peaks below it, because hope is not triumph.

**B: a song, not a score.**
* **Opening:** the plain flute sings the whole theme once, the legend's song. It is the only full statement until the sun returns.
* **The theft:** a frame drum like a heartbeat; drones that darken into the Phrygian as Night rises; the air sucked into silence with no hit (B has no braam).
* **The spark:** one low breath.
* **The road east:** a walking pulse in quarter notes (frame drum and low strings). The drone climbs a fifth with each new fire, strings only, never handed round the orchestra. The flute answers from far away in the stereo depth.
* **The gathering:** every voice converges on D.
* **The sun:** a D add9 bloom, the theme whole in flute and violins together.
* **The hill at dawn:** the flute alone, and the first birdsong in the film.

B is the quietest cut; its loudest moment is the sun.

**C: the epic tradition, and its argument.** Horns, strings, timpani, harp and a pianissimo brass chorale, with original melodies only. C adds a second motif, **the Ring**: the call with its fifth bent into a tritone. It travels through the cut:

| moment | how the Ring motif sounds |
|---|---|
| the forging | born in glass, over anvil strokes in timpani and low bells |
| the Eye | low brass |
| the temptation | whispered, sweet: the seduction has to be beautiful |
| the unmaking | it unwinds back into the true call |

Around it:
* **The fall:** one glass tone falling in pitch.
* **The refusal:** silence, then the flint strikes, then a lone horn calls at the roar.
* **The Run:** horns peak to peak on beacons 1, 3, 5 and 7, driving strings in 3+3+2.
* **The map:** wonder in F major (violins with a solo violin above, harp, an oboe).
* **The council:** a clarinet intones the call; the chorale moves off the beat, never on a vow.
* **The dawn:** D major, the theme whole in the violins in octaves with a solo violin and horns in counterpoint; no crash, no choir. This is C's loudest point, as the tradition asks.
* **The epilogue:** the intro's plain flute (the film's own voice, not Shire pastiche). The Ring motif returns once, pianissimo, and resolves into the answer: the scar, healed in sound.

**Production.** Split `timeline_v2` into A, B and C timelines. The part cache makes the shared first-half material cheap, and every sync point is in the beat sheets above. New SFX:
* A: the gilt surges; the shutters slamming (a muffled iron thud); the globe going to ash (a long low exhale).
* B: the paper theatre, its wind and fire heard as if through paper; the birds.
* C: the Ring's hiss in the snow; the seething of the melt.

# (9) The three biggest risks, and how to test them early

1. **The new character moments could read as clay dolls, which is a death sentence.** These are C's Ring in the snow (16 s on a face) and the scarred palm, A's watcher at first light, and B's sleeping child at dawn. *Test, days 1–2:* one full-resolution still of each, lit exactly as in the film, judged against the blacklist, plus a two-second motion test of F2's hover and withdrawal. Any doll read switches that moment to its fallback staging (profile, silhouette or defocus, as listed in (6)) *before* anything is built on it.
2. **The four signature sequences could look like tech demos, or cost more than they give.** These are FORGES IN THE LIGHT, THE LAND BECOMES THE MAP, THE ROAD EAST and THE THAW. *Test, week 1:* five quarter-resolution stills and a 48-frame half-resolution motion test of each, played between its real neighbouring shots in the cut. Keep or kill by day 5. Each has a named fallback that keeps its story beat, so killing one never breaks a film.
3. **Each cut's central idea has to read with few or no words, while the north star stays felt, never recognized.** A must read as sight, B as the spark changing hands, C as a fall and a refusal. *Test, now:* cut all three films as animatics at the runtimes above. Use existing renders, stills or placeholders for NEW shots, and a temp track re-cut from the v2 stems. Screen each to two or three people who have read nothing and ask three questions: What do the fires do? What was the danger? What changed?
   * Pass: A "let everyone see; they stopped racing alone"; B "brought the sun back"; C "she wouldn't take it".
   * Fail: anyone names a policy, an institution or a country, or calls A "a film about regulation". Then go subtler, never louder.
   * The same animatic tests the pace. If A's relay (178–238 s) drags, cut images, not time: the slowness is the point, and emptiness is not.
