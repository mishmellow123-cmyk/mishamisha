# THE LONG DAWN v3: panel report, STORY AND TASTE

*Writers' room panel, 26 Sep 2026. Config gate at start: GATE PASS.*

**What I read, in full:** `treatments/poet.md`, `dramatist.md`, `essayist.md` and `contrarian.md`, each from its
`claude/treatment-*` branch (each suffixed working branch holds the same file). **There is no mythmaker branch**, so there are four
treatments, not five. I also read the producers' verdict and updates, `BIBLE_V2.md` and `review/text_redteam.md`, and I viewed the review sheets
(`embers_v2`, `embers_C`, `run`, `map_C`, `accord_v2`, `coda_v2`, `globe_v2`, `beacon_before_after`).

**What I judged against:** the producers' notes up to Update 4 and the scope note. Those are: the labs framing; A never says "AI"; B truly
wordless; C truly Tolkien, leaning far more on actual Lord of the Rings; the cuts must diverge in plot and in look; motion-design
ambition; the north star felt and never recognized; pace, not prohibition; the fire as gift *and* as the most dangerous thing ever
made; the race as the tragedy.

## The verdict in one page

1. **Keep three films; do not consolidate A and B.** The room produced two strong, distinct concepts for A and two for B:
   * **A:** the contrarian's **EVERY STEP CLOSER** (the same fire is a glare when raced toward and a dawn when carried) and the
     dramatist's **THE CROSSING** (a roped line of lanterns on a knife-edge). They are close cousins, and both are strong.
   * **B:** the contrarian's **THE KEEPER** (one woman keeps one beacon for sixty years, in one locked frame) and the
     dramatist's **THE LONGEST NIGHT** (three waves of light over one range: dusk, fires, dawn). Neither is A without subtitles.
   * The Bs that *would* argue for consolidation are the ones that reuse A's embers act as their first act: the poet's, the
     essayist's, and the contrarian's own dream prologue. The fix is to strip the embers act out of B, not to merge the films.
2. **Winners:**
   * **A: the contrarian**, with the dramatist's rope, glass lantern, bellows-keeper and two lines grafted in.
   * **B: the contrarian's THE KEEPER**, *on one condition*: its dream prologue is replaced by the dramatist's dusk, climb and
     dead ember, and its dawn by the dramatist's hand-back. If the lifetime shot fails its week-1 test, the fallback is the
     dramatist's B, whole.
   * **C: the contrarian**, the only C that *is* a Tolkien plot (the whole life of a Ring). Steal the essayist's Mirror, cock crow,
     "no Dark Lord" and the Eye's fall, and the dramatist's Moria and Saruman lines.
3. **Ranking of the treatments as wholes:** 1 contrarian · 2 dramatist · 3 essayist · 4 poet. The poet wrote the best single
   scenes in the room (the Ring in the snow, the scar in the palm, the child asleep in the scarf), but its three cuts follow
   draft 1's skeleton almost beat for beat. That is exactly the "repackaging" the producers named.
4. **The one warning for every writer:** all four put "two rivals reach the fire first" into A. That is the north star's most
   recognizable detail (two great powers moving first), and several stagings of it are too legible. Keep one version, seen
   from above, unheld and unscored, or none (§7).

## 2. The poet: the best scenes, the least divergence

**Diagnosis:** exact. "The fire meant too many things at once" is the cleanest account of draft 1 in the room. The rule it
draws, *one meaning of fire per cut*, is good. The trouble is that each cut keeps draft 1's order of events under its new
meaning.

**A · THE WATCH (fire as sight).** A clean mechanism: the trap is made of darkness, so the only undoing is light that takes no
side. The best ideas are about craft rather than story:
* two camera speeds (sprints before the turn, never faster than a walk after it);
* "every cut motivated by an eyeline", so that the edit itself becomes a beacon chain;
* the WATCH TONE, a low D that never stops from the first roar to the last chord;
* the race's tritone healing into a fifth at the moment the forges see each other.

FORGES IN THE LIGHT (the shutters open, the walls fall) gives the turn a picture. What drags A down is that it is draft 1 again
from minute two onward: council, oaths, dawn and the hill handover, in the old order.

**B · THE STOLEN SUN (a solstice myth).** It has a real mythic engine, and the loveliest single cut in the room: the sun's
last spark dies *as* her flint strikes. Its look (shadow-play, cut paper, lateral motion, no faces lit) is the most distinct
look any writer proposed. The ending is perfect: the child asleep in the red scarf at dawn, and the film's first birdsong.
But its first act is the embers act recoloured (the sleeping sun is the thinking fire; the old kingdoms are the towers; Night
is the vortex; the coal hand is the grasp). The gathering is also the accord render, so the skeleton is A's.

**C · THE REFUSAL.** It holds the most powerful sixteen seconds in any C: the Ring falls like a star into the snow, and she
kneels. Its light is on her face, the vision shimmers on the band, her hand hovers and withdraws. The epilogue, a pale
ring-shaped scar in an old palm with a child's mitten laid over it, is the tenderest image in the room. But on the note
that matters most for C it is the thinnest. Beyond draft 1's Ring, Eye, beacons, map, council and eagles it adds three Tolkien
beats: the Ring leaving a bearer, a Faramir-style refusal, and the wound that never heals. Two things also go unexplained:
how the Ring gets from her beacon to the council's hearth, and the physical risk of holding a face for sixteen seconds.

**Best lines:** *And in its light, each saw the others.* (A: explains the metaphor once, late, after the picture has shown it)
· *And every step toward it paid in gold.* (A) · *She lit a beacon instead.* (C: "instead" carries the whole refusal).
**Worst lines:** *In time, the race was over.* (A: a flat verdict stuck onto a wordless dawn, and the red team's "human race"
mishearing risk) · *So we remember how close we came.* It restores a "So" that the red team removed so that *So someone else
lit a beacon* would be the film's only one.

## 3. The dramatist: the best-made B, the richest ink, a crossing used twice

**Diagnosis:** the sharpest of the four on *mechanism*: "The turn had no mechanism… the film never showed why the racers
could now slow down." Every cut answers it.

**A · THE CROSSING.** The rope is the best single allegorical image in the room. A roped team on a knife-edge is *together,
no one ahead, the pace of the slowest, the dangerous threshold crossed* in one physical fact, and nobody has to say any of it.
Other good ideas:
* **Glass lanterns** ("a fire you can finally see into") are the most beautiful felt version of *understood and verified*.
* **The keeper at the bellows** looks toward the next tower, sees only fire, and pumps harder. That is the producers' "each
  afraid the other will get there first", humanized in four seconds, and the fairest portrait of the builders by any writer.
* **The red under-glow** under the cloud sea goes out patch by patch as the line crosses.
* **A spark falling on dry grass**, and no one sees it.

**Weaknesses:**
* It explains itself in the hinge (*…a fire where all could see*).
* It answers its own trap line with a slogan-shaped echo (*In the open, no one had to slow alone.*).
* **THE KEEPERS RISE is the most on-the-nose staging in the room:** two hooded figures climb out of the red cloud from either
  side and tie in with a rope, while two horns play in harmony "for the first time". That is a handshake between two powers
  with a score cue under it.

**B · THE LONGEST NIGHT.** The purest legend in the room, and structurally the most divergent film:
* **Three waves of light across one range:** the sun leaves the peaks, fires catch on the same peaks, and the sun returns
  as each fire pales "as if handing it back".
* **No embers, no council, no future, no words.**
* **The dead ember:** the village's ember in a clay pot greys under her breath at the summit, so she must make fire from
  stone. It is a turn a child can follow and an adult feels.
* **THE HAND-BACK:** each far horn answers once as its beacon pales, then falls silent, until only her cello is left. It is
  the most musical idea in the room.

Its one weakness is scale: it is the gentlest film, with the least dread.

**C · THE RED BOOK.** The deepest well of book-accurate lore, and every item is correct:
* the Three's purposes (understanding, making, healing);
* the One forged in secret in the Mountain;
* Isengard's pits and furnaces and the felled forest;
* Saruman's "use it well" and Boromir's plea;
* the Dwarves who delved too greedily and too deep;
* Isildur;
* the seven beacon-hills;
* the Dawnless Day;
* the eagle bearing the news;
* Sam's gift and the year of plenty;
* the Red Book's last pages.

The two-inks device (the old story in ink; ours burning through the page, then healing) is a real motion-design language. THE
MAP RISES is C's boldest shot. It has two problems:
* **No protagonist:** the heroine is a cameo, so it moves the head more than the heart.
* **It reuses A's showpiece:** THE FELLOWSHIP (nine lanterns roped across a pass) *is* THE CROSSING. The two cuts would share
  their climax image, which rebuilds the very repackaging the producers flagged.

**Best lines:**
* *It could show us anything, except itself.* (A: the gift and the opacity in seven words, the best line in the room)
* *It took longer than anyone wanted.* (A)
* *Every forge that fed it grew rich.* / *Every spark it threw could have burned the world.* (A)
* *They dug deeper every year, for every stroke paid.* (C: the incentive, told as Moria)
* *Even the wise said: let us take it first, and use it well.* (C)

**Worst lines:**
* *They carried the fire on together, at the pace of the smallest.* (C: it states the core message outright, with draft 1's
  blacklisted word "together")
* *In the open, no one had to slow alone.* (A: fails the poster test)
* *And in the dark of a mountain, one forged a Ring to master them all.* (C: a paraphrase of the meme line, one word away
  from it)

## 4. The essayist: the sharpest argument with Tolkien, the wordiest films

**Diagnosis:** good, and the only one to name the orbital sunrise for what it was: "borrowed from a keynote".

**A · NO ONE RUNS (a Marker-style essay about a future custom).** The most original *form* in the room. The settlement our
years reached survives as ritual (fires lit where the next hill can see; carried, never raced; the torch given away before
dawn), and the film decodes the custom by showing the history. The **stills-then-motion** device is a genuine visual identity:
the future is seen as stills in which only the fires move, and the future moves at last in the dawn. **THE DEAD HILL** is the
single most frightening image in any treatment: the film's own opening frame, dead, with ash falling, no ring and a dark
Moon, over *Or there might be no one left to light them.* It turns the frame's "might" into the stakes, which answers the
contrarian's objection that a far-future frame guarantees survival.

The costs:
* **It is an essay:** seventeen lines, and customs phrased as aphorisms (*No one runs.*), which is where slogans breed.
* **THE TORCH RACE** of ember runners risks reading as an action-movie crowd simulation.

**B · THE OPEN HAND.** Lovely parts:
* the **travelling-spark match cuts**;
* the **geometry rule**: the race climbs vertically and the answer spreads horizontally;
* **THE WAITING**: she keeps her fire alone while the stars wheel, and the far horn's ANSWER is heard for the first time;
* **HER DAWN**: she warms her open hands in the sun as she did at the fire.

But its first 65 seconds are the glyphs, the thinking fire, the towers, the crown, the race, the storm and the grasp. That is
the allegory's own picture with the captions removed, which is the exact failure of draft 1's B.

**C · THE LAST PAGES (the Ring without a Dark Lord).** The most intelligent C. The refrain *In the old story…* marks the
three places our world is harder than Tolkien's:
* **No Dark Lord to blame:** the Eye forms out of the race itself.
* **No Crack of Doom to go back to.**
* **No bearer who can be trusted at the brink** (even Frodo fails), and that is why NO SINGLE HAND SHALL HOLD IT.

It is the best statement anyone made of the producers' labs framing, in Tolkien's own terms. Its deep cuts are superb and
correct:
* **Galadriel's Mirror** ("things that yet may be") as the vertigo of futures;
* **the cock crowing at the Gate** at the darkest hour, "recking nothing of wizardry or war";
* **Elrond's** *small hands… while the eyes of the great are elsewhere*;
* **the Eye falling** as Barad-dûr falls.

The costs:
* **The heroine is a cameo.** Like the dramatist's C, it moves the head more than the heart.
* **The refrain can turn into a lecture.**
* **The ending repeats:** all three of the essayist's cuts end on the same hill handover.

**Best lines:**
* *Or there might be no one left to light them.* (A)
* *In ours, there was no Dark Lord.* (C: the producers' "neither heroes nor villains" in six words)
* *At dawn, they let the fires go out.* (A: the gift so ample the watch can end)
* *And someone lit a beacon, while the eyes of the great were elsewhere.* (C)

**Worst lines:**
* *In the old story, the Ring first appears as a party trick.* (C: "party trick" invites the laugh the essayist fears.
  In the older book the Ring first appears in the dark, not at a party.)
* *Now each could see the others. No one had to run.* (A: the second sentence is poster-shaped)
* *There was light enough in it for everyone.* (A: sentimental)

## 5. The contrarian: the strongest concepts, one flaw per cut

**Diagnosis:** the most useful in the room, because it questions the premises. It asks whether a legend told a lifetime
later guarantees survival, whether a sunrise nobody causes is an ending, and whether a single night can hold decades of
coordination. Its answer, three films on **three time-scales** (a night, a lifetime, an age), is the room's best solution
to "three distinct films".

**A · EVERY STEP CLOSER (one night, embers and black).** The tightest central idea: *the same fire is a glare when you race
toward it and a dawn when you carry it together*. Everything obeys one physics of light and speed:
* **FALSE DAWN:** a cold glow at midnight, beyond the ranges, where no sun should rise. It is the hype, in one image.
* **THE EDGE:** a crater opens around the fire, and the towers lean over its rim. Each surge *gilds the nearest* while the
  rim crumbles under them. This is the producers' "a bomb that prints gold" exactly, shown and never said. It is the best
  image of Update 4 in the room.
* **DARK ADAPTATION:** out of the glare, the stars return, and small lights can be seen.
* **There is no first fire:** the pull-back finds every ridge catching at once. It is the cleanest answer to "no lone hero".
* **THE CARRYING:** the procession never walks faster than the carried fire lights the path. *No faster than we can see*
  becomes a physical law of walking at night.
* **The dawn:** the carried fire and the first light become **one light**. It rises over the terraces, orchards and river
  that the fire drew in its first breath, now real. The dawn is *caused*, and the abundance was promised at minute one.

The weaknesses are small but real:
* *merchants* (a faint sneer) and *builders* (the one modern-literal noun);
* the fire carried in "an open iron **cage**", which a knowing viewer can read as containment, a real-world mechanism;
* a crowd of 200–400 bearers, which is costly and risks reading as LED dots;
* the carved oaths repeated from C.

**B · THE KEEPER (one lifetime, stone and sky).** The most moving idea anyone brought, and a proven form for an animated
short (Frédéric Back's *The Man Who Planted Trees*).
* **THE FIRST NIGHT:** she keeps a beacon; nothing answers; at a grey dawn she sets one stone on the cairn.
* **THE LIFETIME SHOT:** one locked frame for sixty years, one stone a year. Under it a chaconne whose ground bass steps
  *down* a degree each year while nothing answers.
* **The turn:** a child on the far peak points at her light. The bass turns and climbs, the ANSWER is heard for the first
  time, and later the grown child becomes the shepherd who answers (a perfect reuse of an existing render).
* **The world:** travellers take flame down the mountain; valleys light under the cloud sea; she greys.
* **The dawn:** the first gold dawn, after sixty grey ones. "The future is built, not arrived at."

Its flaw is fatal as written: **the first 73 seconds are a dream of the racing road.** That is the glyphs, the thinking
fire, the towers, the edge, the claw and the ash. It re-imports the allegory into the one cut that must be for its own sake,
and it makes B's motive an argument ("refusing the racing road") rather than a feeling. §6 fixes this.

**C · THE LAST PAGES (one age, ink and gold).** The only C that is a Tolkien *plot* rather than a Tolkien *commentary*: the
whole life of a Ring, with our difference at each joint.
1. Forged in the dark.
2. Seized by one hand (Isildur).
3. Betraying that hand and lost.
4. Found by chance in the dark by someone small (Bilbo's riddles, here at the second flint strike).
5. The call for aid (Gondor's beacons).
6. A council that refuses the Ring and keeps the fire, and is *not hasty* (the Entmoot).
7. The bearer who cannot let it go at the brink (Isildur and Frodo).
8. Grace: the fire everyone lit takes it, where the book had Gollum.
9. The year of plenty (the mallorn in the Party Field).
10. The Havens: the coast beacons lit in farewell, and she raises a small light in answer from the stern.

The best idea in all four Cs is this: *the bearer gets a swift sunrise; those who stay get the long dawn.* The title is
earned inside Tolkien's own words.

The costs:
* **The runtime** is 6:40 with three codas after the dawn (plenty, the road west, the Havens), though the treatment itself
  warns about "too many endings".
* **The council's silhouettes** (the tall one with long hair, the braided beard, the wizard's hat and staff) tip into cosplay.
* *They were not hasty.* may draw a knowing chuckle.
* **A loose statement of the rule:** "no hand holding it can let it go" is not strictly true. Bilbo and Sam both gave it up;
  the law holds *at the Fire*, which is where the film uses it.

**Best lines:**
* *At first it looked like morning.* (A)
* *No one dared to be second.* (A: the race in five words)
* *There would have been no morning after it.* (A: dread in the conditional, from after)
* *No one got there first.* (A: the best last line in the room; it inverts the race)
* *So she called for aid.* (C)
* *The one who carried it could not let it go.* (C)

**Worst lines:**
* *In that light, the builders could see each other.* (A: "builders" is the one noun from our world, and the poet's version
  is better)
* *The merchants raced toward it first. Then the kings.* (A: arch)
* *They were not hasty.* (C: test it for a laugh)

## 6. Which version of each cut wins, and exactly what to steal

### A: the contrarian's EVERY STEP CLOSER wins

It wins because it has one physics (light and speed) and the best image of the incentive (THE EDGE). Its dawn is *caused*
(the carried fire becomes the light), and its first and last lines bracket the argument: *At first it looked like
morning.* … *No one got there first.* The dramatist's CROSSING is a close second, and the two converge independently on
a line of lights crossing a knife-edge above the cloud sea. When two writers invent the same image separately, keep it.

**Steal, beat by beat:**
1. **THE KEEPER AT THE BELLOWS** (dramatist A8, 4 s), inside THE EDGE between *Every step closer paid.* and *No one dared to
   be second.* In one gilded tower's window, an ember silhouette pumps on the beat. It turns toward the next tower, sees only
   glare, and pumps harder; gold spills. The builders get their one human moment: afraid, not greedy.
2. **THE DEAD VALLEY** (the essayist's DEAD HILL, translated). Between the IMPACT and the black, hold the valley that THE
   PROMISE drew (terraces, orchards, river) for four seconds as cold cinder under falling ash. Then comes the black, with the
   ember that does not die, and *There would have been no morning after it.* Now the ruin is a picture and the dawn's valley
   is its answer: the widest spread of futures, in one composition shown twice.
3. **The glass lantern replaces the iron cage** (dramatist's glass lanterns). The fire is carried in a large lantern of iron
   and glass on long poles, with the fire visible inside it. *No one could see inside it* becomes *now you can*, and the
   containment reading disappears. Use the contrarian's own fallback for the crowd: about forty bearers, larger and closer,
   not four hundred dots.
4. **THE ROPE on the knife-edge** (dramatist S24). The bearers are tied in along the whole line: a working rope, shown in a
   wide, in many hands. Do *not* take THE KEEPERS RISE's two hooded figures tying in, in medium-wide.
5. **The red under-glow** (dramatist) beneath the cloud sea goes out patch by patch as the line crosses.
6. **The eyeline rule** (poet). After the turn, every cut is motivated by someone seeing a fire, so the edit itself becomes a
   beacon chain.
7. **The watch tone** (poet): a low D in the cellos from the roar of her fire to the last chord. Add the poet's tritone
   healing into a fifth at *each saw the others*, alongside the contrarian's thinking cycle slowing to quarter notes.
8. **No carved oaths in A** (the dramatist's call). The rim scene stays wordless, and the carrying is the accord in motion.
   The vows live in C, where carved vows belong. This alone removes A's most policy-shaped words.

**A's on-screen text after the steals** (thirteen lines, about 75 words):

| # | line | source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *At first it looked like morning.* | contrarian |
| 2 | *We had fed it everything we had ever written.* | contrarian |
| 3 | *And it began to think.* | all |
| 4 | *It could show us anything, except itself.* | dramatist (over THE PROMISE; replaces *No one could see inside it.*) |
| 5 | *Whoever held it alone would hold the world.* | draft 1 |
| 6 | *First the forges raced for it. Then the kingdoms.* | dramatist (replaces the merchants and kings) |
| 7 | *Every step closer paid.* | contrarian |
| 8 | *No one dared to be second.* | contrarian |
| 9 | *There would have been no morning after it.* | contrarian (on black) |
| 10 | *So the rest of us lit fires, to see by.* | contrarian |
| 11 | *And in their light, each saw the others.* | poet ("its" becomes "their" to follow line 10; replaces *the builders*) |
| 12 | *It took longer than anyone wanted.* | dramatist (over the knife-edge) |
| 13 | *No one got there first.* | contrarian (last words; then the title) |

What this costs: *Who lit the first one?*, the red team's protected last line, leaves the film with the hill frame. It is
answered in the picture instead (there is no first fire: every ridge catches at once), and *No one got there first.* does
its job without a child to ask the question.

### B: the contrarian's THE KEEPER wins, with its prologue replaced

THE KEEPER wins because the lifetime shot is the strongest idea the room produced for a wordless film. It is fidelity
without reward, a stone a year, the child who saw her fire growing up to answer it, and a chaconne whose bass steps down a
degree each year until someone answers. But the dream of the racing road has to go. B must owe nothing to A.

**The rebuilt B**, about 5:00, wordless, with no embers act:
1. **The dramatist's opening** (B1–B4, about 57 s):
   * **DUSK:** the light leaves the peaks one by one, with a cello descending the ANSWER, one note per peak.
   * **THE CLIMB:** she carries the village's ember in a clay pot, the only warm point in a blue world.
   * **THE DEAD EMBER:** it greys under her breath at the summit.
2. **The match cut** (the poet's and the essayist's device): the ember's last red eye becomes her first flint spark. Then the
   first fire and the reveal, moonlit, with no red.
3. **The contrarian's middle**, whole:
   * THE FIRST NIGHT: nothing answers, a grey dawn, one stone.
   * THE LIFETIME SHOT, with its chaconne.
   * The child on the far peak who points, and the grown child who becomes the shepherd.
   * Travellers taking flame down; valleys lighting under the cloud; the sky's ring closing arc by arc; her hair turning
     white.
4. **The night the whole range is alight** (the Run, the far world, the globe). Optionally, make it one move with the
   essayist's **THE ASCENT**, from her summit to orbit. That shot lost its home in A (A now ends on the ground), and in B it
   is sixty years summed in a single camera move.
5. **The sixtieth dawn is the dramatist's HAND-BACK**, seen from behind her. The far beacons pale in the order the light
   reaches them, and each far horn answers once and falls silent, until only her cello is left. Inside it, the essayist's
   gesture: she draws her old hands from her sleeves and opens them to the sun, as she did to the fire.
6. **The last image is the poet's.** The child who climbed with her this last time is asleep against her in the red scarf.
   The sun reaches them, and the film's first birdsong starts. She touches the child's shoulder; the child wakes and looks
   at the sun. The title kindles in the dawn sky and fades into the light (the dramatist's B title).

**Why the swap works:**
* **Motive:** the rebuilt B has a motive a child can tell back: *once the fire nearly died; she never let it die again.*
* **Origin:** B becomes the legend of how the festival of fires began, which shares the world with A and C naturally.
* **Dread:** it lives in the legend's own terms: the dead ember, the first unanswered night, the chaconne falling year on year.
* **The torch handover** is retired from every cut. It stays in reserve as B's fallback if the new dawn pose reads as a doll.

**The fallback for B:** if the lifetime shot's 45-second greybox reads as a slideshow, make the dramatist's THE LONGEST NIGHT
whole. Its dusk and hand-back are already the rebuilt B's first and last acts, so nothing is wasted.

### C: the contrarian's THE LAST PAGES wins

It wins because it is the only C told *through* The Lord of the Rings' own plot, in the book's own order: a bearer, a fall,
a finding, a call, a council, a failure at the brink, grace, plenty, the Havens. It keeps the one argument with Tolkien that
draft 1 found (*This fire could not be unmade*) and adds a second that is truer still to the book: the bearer fails, and the
many save it. The dramatist's RED BOOK is the richest in lore and the essayist's is the sharpest argument, so C takes the
most steals.

**Steal, beat by beat:**
1. **The race lines, retold as Tolkien's two tragedies:**
   * Before the race, replace *And every kingdom and every forge wanted it.* with the essayist's pivot: *There was no Dark
     Lord.* / *Only every forge in every kingdom, racing to make it first.* This is the labs framing in the book's terms.
     Drop the essayist's "In ours", since C has not yet said "in the old story".
   * Over THE EDGE, the dramatist's *They dug deeper every year, for every stroke paid.* The crater becomes Moria's deep, and
     the gilded rim its bright vein.
   * Then keep the contrarian's *Each said: better us than our enemies.*, gone before the Eye opens.
2. **THE MIRROR** (essayist) replaces the plain cracking world. It is Galadriel's basin: the lands burning in it, a drop, a
   golden dawn for one breath, then fire again and the Eye rising in the water. C gets its own vertigo, unlike A's plunge.
3. **The fall and the find, merged:**
   * **The fall** (the poet's staging): the Ring tumbles out of the black through cloud as a gold glint and strikes the snow
     by her cairn with a hiss and a plume of steam.
   * **The cock crow** (essayist): far away in the dark, a cock crows once. The morning comes regardless.
   * **The find** (contrarian): at her second flint strike the spark shows the band in the melted hollow.
   * **The vision** (poet): when she opens her hand, the vision shimmers on the band for two seconds (the forges bowing, the
     Eye) before she closes her fist.
4. **One morph only.** The contrarian's LIVING INK RUN ends in the poet's THE LAND BECOMES THE MAP (the ranges flatten into
   the parchment sheet, and the beacons become flame glyphs), landing on map_C's first framing. The dramatist's MAP RISES
   is the same machinery reversed; keep it as the fallback, not as a second morph.
5. **The council:** hoods, one grey wide-brimmed hat seen from above, and one small figure (her), as the essayist stages it.
   Drop the contrarian's roster of the long-haired one, the braided beard and the staff: that is costume.
6. **After the white, THE EYE FALLS** (essayist E11). The iris flies apart into embers, the vortex unwinds, the towers sink
   to embers and ash drifts down, played as an exhale. Then the dawn.
7. **The Ring motif never cadences until the Ring is taken by the fire.** Then it resolves into D, once (the dramatist's
   rule, on the contrarian's corrupted call).
8. **The endings:**
   * Keep THE YEAR OF PLENTY to 15 seconds or less: one field, one tree.
   * Cut THE ROAD WEST; her bound hand is seen at the Havens instead, as she raises the small light from the stern.
   * End on the empty sea and the paling east, with *The last pages were left for us.*
   * Target about 6:00, not 6:40.
9. **Keep the contrarian's wording of the last line** (not Frodo's exact *The last pages are for you.*). No one is handed a
   book on screen, so "for you" would address the audience. If the producers want the exact quotation, add the
   dramatist's hearth frame, and let an old hand give a child the book at the very end.

**Watch in the read-through:** *They were not hasty.* (a Treebeard chuckle would kill the council; if it gets one, cut the
line and let the slow carving of the vows carry it) and *There was no Dark Lord.* (it must land as grief, not as a
debating point).

## 7. The scope question, answered explicitly

**Does A have a strong, distinct concept? Yes: two.**
* The contrarian's EVERY STEP CLOSER (the winner).
* The dramatist's THE CROSSING.

The poet's THE WATCH (sight) and the essayist's NO ONE RUNS (a future custom) are also sound. The essayist's stills device
is worth keeping in a drawer.

**Does B have a strong, distinct concept? Yes: two.**
* The contrarian's THE KEEPER, once the dream is removed.
* The dramatist's THE LONGEST NIGHT, which is excellent as written.

Both are legends for their own sake, and neither shares a plot with any A.

**So: three films, not two.** Consolidation would throw away the single most moving idea in the room, the lifetime shot,
and the purest one, the three waves of light. What looked like an A/B overlap in the drafts was never a shortage of ideas.
It was writers reusing the embers act as B's first act, and that is fixed by removing it from B.

## 8. The north star: what shows through, and what to cut

The producers' caution is that it must be felt and never recognized. These are every staging detail in the room that a
knowing viewer could decode as a real mechanism, most exposed first:

| two torches from opposite sides reach the hearth first | essayist A, contrarian A | **keep at most once** (A's rim), from above, unheld, unscored. If any viewer names two countries, randomize the order. |

## 10. Tests to add to the writers' own

1. **B's swap, in week 1.** Cut a 60-second animatic from the dusk to the dead ember, the first night and 20 seconds of
   lifetime greybox. Ask cold viewers: *why does she keep going back?* It passes if they say something like "so the fire never
   dies again" or "she promised". It fails if anyone asks what the dream meant, because there should be nothing to decode.
2. **A's decoder test** (the contrarian's), run with the steals in place: the rope, the glass lantern, and the rim with or
   without the two first torches. Any viewer who names a real mechanism or a pair of countries sends that detail subtler.
3. **C's laugh test** on the three new or risky lines: *There was no Dark Lord.* · *They were not hasty.* · *They dug deeper
   every year, for every stroke paid.* Read them to two Tolkien readers and two strangers. A knowing laugh cuts the line; a
   nod keeps it.

## 11. Ranking

**Treatments as wholes:**
1. **Contrarian.** The strongest concepts and the only genuinely three-way plot divergence, and it wins all three cuts.
   It has one flaw per cut (B's dream, C's endings and costumes, A's cage), and each is fixable by a steal.
2. **Dramatist.** The best-made B, the richest Tolkien lore, the rope, and the best line in the room. It is held back by
   sharing its crossing between A and C, and by the most legible north-star staging.
3. **Essayist.** The sharpest argument with Tolkien, THE DEAD HILL, the Mirror and the cock crow. It is held back by its
   wordiness, by a B that is A's picture without captions, and by one ending used three times.
4. **Poet.** The best scenes (the Ring in the snow, the scar, the child in the scarf) and the best editing ideas (the
   eyeline chain, the watch tone). It is held back because all three cuts keep draft 1's order of events.

**By cut:**
* **A:** contrarian › dramatist › essayist › poet
* **B:** contrarian (with the swap) › dramatist › poet › essayist
* **C:** contrarian › dramatist › essayist › poet
