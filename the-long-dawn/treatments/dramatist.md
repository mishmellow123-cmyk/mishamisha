# THE LONG DAWN v3: three films (treatment, the dramatist)

Writers' room, 26 Sep 2026. This answers `review/PRODUCERS_VERDICT_DRAFT1.md`, the note on cut C, and the four
producer updates that arrived during the room: visual divergence, ambition, the north star, the core message and the
stakes. I studied the bibles, the current text and edit, every department's notes, both red-team reports, all
review sheets, and the draft-1 storyboards of A and C.

## Why draft 1 didn't land

1. **The fire meant three things, and nothing linked them.** It was the danger (the thinking fire), the answer (the
   beacons) and the reward (the sun). So "someone lit a beacon" couldn't explain why a race stopped. That is the
   incoherence the producers felt.
2. **The captions carried the allegory and the picture carried the myth.** Literal nouns ("the companies… then the
   countries", four policy oaths) sat on abstract images. The text read as on the nose and the images as vague.
3. **The turn had no mechanism.** A beacon was lit, the world answered, a council agreed. The film never showed why
   the racers could now slow down.
4. **It hurried.** Nine ideas in two minutes, one shot each.
5. **A and C were one film in two costumes.** They had the same spine, the same turns and the same ending.

**The fix in one sentence.** There is one fire, and every story is about *how it is carried*: hidden, alone and as
fast as possible (the race), or in the open, hand to hand, only as fast as the next watcher can see (beacons). Nobody
in any cut tries to put the fire out. Each cut tells a different story in that shared grammar.

# A · THE CROSSING (the allegory; on screen: THE LONG DAWN)

## A(1) Logline
A lifetime from now, a child asks why the hills are lit. The answer is the night we made a fire that could think. We
raced it through the dark until it nearly burned the world, and then we learned to carry it in the open, together,
only as fast as we could see.

## A(2) The central idea, and why it holds from first frame to last
**One fire, carried two ways.** The fire is the new power. It is the greatest gift anyone has made, and on the racing
path the most dangerous. No one in the film tries to put it out. The whole question is how it is carried.

- **In the dark, alone, as fast as possible: the forges.** Every stroke of the bellows pays in gold, and every spark
  could burn the world. Walls of their own fire keep the forges from seeing each other, so each one races: it can't
  see the other slow.
- **In the open, hand to hand, at the pace of sight: beacons.** A beacon is a fire put where everyone can see it, and
  a chain of them moves only as fast as each watcher can see the last. Nobody can run ahead in a beacon chain. That
  is the whole politics of the film, and the film never says it.

| key image | what it means (felt, never explained) |
|---|---|
| the thinking fire: ice-white, breathing too slowly and too evenly | the new power, unlike any fire before |
| the gift: its light reaching into the dark and showing us things | the promise is real: understanding, abundance |
| looking into the fire: filaments knotted past reading | it shows us everything except itself |
| gold running down the towers | every step closer pays |
| a spark falling on dry grass far below | the danger is total |
| red walls between the towers; later, a cloud sea lit red from beneath | the dark in which racers can't see each other |
| the vertiginous fall onto a cracking world; the grasp | the two ruins: everything burns, or one hand holds it all |
| the ember that dies on black | how close we came: everything, forever |
| a fire on a summit above the cloud | the turn: making it visible, from outside the race |
| watch-fires on every peak | everyone watching, openly |
| two white lanterns climbing out of the red cloud and meeting | the two greatest racers stepping into the open |
| the rope; more lanterns rising to tie in | together; no one can run ahead; others join |
| glass lanterns, the fire visible inside | a fire you can finally see into |
| the crossing under a wheeling sky | slow on purpose: the dangerous threshold, crossed |
| the lanterns warming to gold as the sun reaches them | the fire's promise, shared by everyone |
| the torch put in the child's hands | the watch never ends |

**Why the turn is inevitable.** The film states the trap in one line, *None would slow alone*, and shows its cause in
one image: the racers can't see each other. So the turn has to be sight. Someone outside the race puts a fire where
all can see it. Everyone answers. In that light the racers can see each other, and they can slow together. The next
image can only be the crossing, because they don't stop, they carry it. Its end can only be the dawn, the light
everyone shares. Nothing arrives from outside the metaphor.

## A(3) Runtime: 5:08
It needs its whole chain: gift, incentive, danger, trap, turn, crossing, dawn. Draft 1 squeezed that chain into two
minutes, and the producers called it rushed and forced. The form carries the idea. The first half accelerates: cuts
land on the beat and the rhythm doubles. After the turn the film slows, never speeds up again, and ends on its longest
takes. The length is the argument: it takes longer, and that's the point. Longer than about 5:30 and the allegory
starts to preach; shorter than about 4:30 and the crossing can't feel long.

## A(8) Why it isn't forced, and why it isn't on the nose
- **One metaphor, held all the way through.** Draft 1 asked fire to be the danger, the answer and the reward at once.
  Here there is one fire, and the story is only ever about how it is carried. The beacons don't fight the fire: they
  are the same fire, carried in the open. The dawn doesn't replace it: it is what the fire becomes when it is shared.
- **The balance is set on purpose.** One literal anchor ("everything we had ever written") makes the mapping
  unmistakable; every other line uses the myth's own nouns (forges, kingdoms, sparks, fire). Every policy idea is an
  image and never a word: sight, the rope, glass lanterns, pace. A viewer from our field reads every beat. Anyone
  else sees a legend about fire and people.
- **The builders are neither heroes nor villains.** They are shown rich, trapped and frightened. In their one close
  moment, the keeper at the bellows, one of them wants to stop and can't. Then they are shown relieved. The turn comes
  from outside them, but they carry the fire; they are not stopped. The two lanterns meet as equals, and nobody is
  thanked.
- **The dread is honest.** A spark on dry grass, a fall onto a cracking world, one hand closing, an ember going out:
  four images of ruin, and two kinds of it (everything burns, or one hand holds it all). The dawn is earned by a night
  the film makes long.
- **Cringe guards.** No hands joined, no speeches, no crowds cheering, nothing carved or captioned at the climax. The
  rope is a working tool on a dangerous ridge, not a symbol held up to camera. Nobody raises a torch; no face is lit
  in triumph. The last word is a child's question.
- **The north star is in the shape only.** This mapping is for the producers and never appears on screen:

  | the producers' ideal outcome | its trace in the myth |
  |---|---|
  | the work made public | a fire lit where all can see |
  | the world watching the frontier | watch-fires on every summit, fed all night |
  | two great rivals move first, and others join | two lanterns meet at the saddle; more rise to tie in |
  | many builders moving together, none racing ahead | the roped line |
  | deliberately slowed until it can be understood and checked | glass lanterns; the pace of the slowest; *It took longer than anyone wanted.* |
  | public pressure is what turns it | the answering fires come *before* the keepers move |
  | power stays spread out, people stay in control, everyone flourishes | twelve small flames instead of one fist, carried by hands; sun on every peak |

# B · THE LONGEST NIGHT (the legend; wordless; on screen: THE LONG DAWN only)

## B(1) Logline
On the longest night of the year the light leaves the mountains. A young woman climbs into the dark with the last
ember of her village, finds it dead, strikes new fire on the highest peak, and keeps it burning with everyone who
answers until the sun comes back to take it from her.

## B(2) The central idea, and why it holds
**Fire keeps faith with the sun.** When the light leaves the world, people carry it across the dark, peak to peak and
hand to hand, and give it back at dawn. There is no allegory to serve, no race and no thinking fire. This is the myth
that A and C are built on, told for its own sake. The whole film is **three waves of light across one mountain
range**:

1. **Dusk.** The sunlight leaves the peaks one by one, the lowest first, until only her summit holds a red point, and
   then it goes out.
2. **Night.** Fires catch on those same peaks one by one: hers first, then the shepherd's, then the chain, then the
   world.
3. **Dawn.** The sunlight returns peak by peak, and each fire pales as the light reaches it, as if handing it back.

| key image | what it means |
|---|---|
| the light leaving each peak | light is not guaranteed; the night is real |
| the clay pot with the village's ember | you carry what you have into the dark |
| the ember going grey in her hands | the old fire can't be carried across; new fire must be made |
| three strikes, a breath, a catch | one person, the cold, and the work of it |
| a far figure turning to light the next cairn | she was seen, and she is not alone |
| the chain across the range, the world | fire moves only as fast as sight, and everyone joins in their own way |
| the vigil under the wheeling sky | keeping the light is long, slow work |
| each beacon paling as the sun reaches it | the fire's job was to carry the light across the dark, and it is done |
| threads of torches winding down to the valleys | the light goes home, to every hearth |

**Why the turn is inevitable.** The legend is a symmetry. The second wave exists to bridge the first and the third,
so the story needs no argument, only an act. The single turn is physical: the ember she carried is dead, so she has to
make fire from stone. Everything after is the answer to that act.

## B(3) Runtime: 3:42
One night, three waves. A wordless film holds attention for about four minutes. The vigil needs half a minute to feel
like a night, and the dawn needs forty seconds to hand back every fire. Any shorter and the waves collapse into
montage; any longer and the silence starts to drift.

## B(8) Why it isn't forced or on the nose
- **There is no allegory to balance.** Every beat is a physical act (climb, fail, strike, answer, keep, hand back)
  that a child could follow. The legend works as a legend.
- **It resonates without pointing.** The fire carried together through the dark, only as fast as sight, and given
  back to the light at dawn is the core idea of A and C in its oldest form. Nothing in B asks to be decoded.
- **It diverges from A and C in every structural choice.** B has no race, no vision, no council, no future frame, no
  question and no handover. Its protagonist's arc is endurance; its climax is a giving-back. Visually it is the only
  cut without embers or ink: natural light, one landscape, one night.
- **Cringe guards.** She never raises the torch or poses. At the dawn she simply stands. No slow motion, no swelling
  choir, no face in close-up at the climax: we see her from behind, against the sun.

# C · THE RED BOOK (the Tolkien cut; on screen: THE LONG DAWN)

## C(1) Logline
By a winter hearth, an old woman reads a child the old story of the Ring, until she reaches the page where it can no
longer guide us: this fire cannot be unmade. The new chapter is written by many hands: a beacon, old enemies
answering, a council that melts the Ring in a fire every people fed, and a fellowship that carries the fire on at the
pace of the smallest. Its last pages are left for the child.

## C(2) The central idea, and why it holds
**The old story teaches us everything except its ending.** In *The Lord of the Rings* the Ring is unmade in the fire
where it was forged, and the power of the Three passes out of the world with it. Ours can't end that way. Our fire
cannot be unmade, and it shouldn't be: it is the gift the old smiths dreamed of, to understand, to make and to heal.
So C separates the two things the old story fused:

- **the fire** is the gift, and it goes on;
- **the Ring** is the dream of mastering the fire alone, and that is what must be unmade.

The new chapter unmakes the Ring with the old story's own means, given a new end: a beacon lit to call old allies,
old enemies answering, a council where every people refuses the Ring, and a fellowship that walks at the pace of its
smallest. The Ring melts in a fire everyone fed, and the fire walks on.

**Two inks.** The old story is drawn in ink on the pages of a red book, in the plain, strong line of Tolkien's own
illustrations. Our story is fire. Wherever ours breaks through the old, the page scorches and burns open onto the
ember world; when the new chapter is written, the book heals, and the last illustration is in gold leaf. The
typography follows: on the page, lines write themselves in ink; on black, they kindle as fire-letters, the way the
Ring's inscription shows only in fire.

## C(8) Why it isn't forced, and why it isn't on the nose
- **Every reference carries weight.** Each Tolkien beat does one step of our argument (see the table in C(2)), so a
  reader of the book gets a second layer, and a viewer who has never read it still follows a clear story: a book, a
  gap, a new chapter. Nothing is there only to be recognised.
- **Loving, not parody.** It uses the book's deep grain (the Three's purposes, the Dwarves' greed, the seven
  beacon-hills, the year of plenty, the Red Book) rather than its memes. It avoids "you shall not pass", "my
  precious", "fly", eagles as rescue, the Celtic flute (the critics' M9), and any named character. Tolkien's own
  illustrations set the drawing style, and the drawings stay simple, graphic and sincere.
- **It takes Tolkien's lesson rather than bending it.** The framing critic's objection to draft 1 was that it made
  the whole technology the Ring and then let a committee share the Ring. C separates them: the Council refuses the
  Ring exactly as Elrond's council did, and it keeps the fire, which was never the Ring.
- **It takes no side.** The Eye stands over every tower. "Old enemies" replaces "the free peoples". The red glow lies
  under the cloud all around. There is no east or west except the sunrise.
- **The allegory is balanced.** Three lines carry the mapping to our moment (every word we wrote; every kingdom and
  forge; every stroke paid). Everything else is Tolkien's world doing our work. The north star appears here as a
  council and a fellowship, which is exactly the book's own shape for hope: many peoples, a hard choice, a long walk,
  no single hero.
- **It ends as the book ends.** The story is handed on, and the last pages are blank: sober hope, not triumph.

# (7) Music

**Shared across the three scores** (original melodies only):
- **The Beacon theme**: the CALL (D–A–D, rising) and the ANSWER (falling). It is the sound of fire passing from one
  person to the next.
- **The thinking fire**: the glass FM cycle and the IGNITION chord (B♭maj9♯11). Its arc is the film's harmonic
  metaphor. It is cold and glassy at the ignition and warms note by note as the fire becomes something shared, until
  it glows inside the dawn chord.
- **The breaths**: silence before the race, the suck into the IMPACT, and 275 ms before every dawn.
- **The rules the critics earned**: 72 BPM, with beacons on the beat. The music never hits a beacon, an oath or a vow
  (the effects own them). No choir, real or synthetic; no organ manuals; no cymbal victory. Every dawn is a bloom out
  of silence, with awe coming from harmony rather than melody and a peak below the race's loudness.

**A: the pace, made audible.** The sound world of draft 1's first half: strings, low brass, piano, the FM glass and
taiko.

| section | music |
|---|---|
| frame and kindling (0–60 s) | The flute's call; the glass arpeggio; the ignition bloom; the arpeggio warms for three bars during the gift, then cools. |
| forges and race (60–113) | A heartbeat pulse, then the taiko race. The tempo never changes, but the rhythm doubles every two bars (quarters, eighths, sixteenths, thirty-seconds): the film's only accelerando. The drums cut out at the fall; a riser; the suck. |
| silence (113–128) | Solo piano, the call in fragments. |
| the turn (128–165) | A low D; the catch on B♭/D; the roar opens strings and low brass; a solo horn calls and a far horn answers. From the Run on, one steady pulse at 72 that never changes again: the pace we can see by. |
| keepers and crossing (165–220) | Two horns play call and answer together for the first time. Then a walking ostinato (low pizzicato strings, one step per beat) with the call inside the chord in the violas. The harmony moves only when the line passes a watch-fire, so every change is rare and earned. |
| dawn (220–253) | 275 ms of silence, then a bloom on D add9 with the glass cycle glowing warm inside it, no tune for 80 frames, and the call late on cellos in unison. |
| coda (253–308) | The piano asks the call; the answer comes during the handover, with the flint echo; the answering fires are string swells in one family; the final chord. |

**B: one voice, and then everyone's.** No drums, no FM glass (there is no thinking fire in B), no piano.
- **Dusk:** a low D, and a solo cello descending the ANSWER, one note for each peak that goes dark.
- **The climb and the dead ember:** the cello's last note held under the wind, then only wind.
- **The first fire and the relay:** the roar opens the strings; a horn calls; far horns answer; the call travels from
  peak to peak, near and far, left and right, so the relay can be heard.
- **The world:** one voice per place, all in one family (horns and strings), the harmony moving by thirds.
- **The vigil:** a very slow low-string chorale and the solo cello, which is her voice.
- **The hand-back:** a bloom out of silence and the call, late, on the cello. As each far beacon pales, its horn
  answers once and falls silent, until only her cello is left. B ends the softest of the three.

**C: the epic tradition, heard from inside a book.** Original melodies only: horns passed from peak to peak,
strings, timpani, and a pianissimo brass chorale in place of a choir.
- **Two sound worlds.** The book is chamber music (damped harp, clarinet, solo violin); the fire is the orchestra.
  Every burn-through is a change of orchestration, not a hit.
- **The RING motif (new).** Four notes in a closed loop that never cadences. It is heard at the forging, under the
  race and the wise, alone in the silence, and circling under the Council. It resolves, once, into D as the Ring melts
  (236–246 s). That resolution is C's emotional climax, and it comes before the dawn.
- **The deep:** an anvil-like tick on every beat, dividing faster and faster: the pay.
- **The turn:** a lone horn cries the call on the roar and a second horn answers from the far peak; in the Run, horns
  sound on beacons 1, 3, 5 and 7 only.
- **The map:** F major wonder, with the theme in the violins, damped harp and horns answering.
- **The dawn:** a timpani roll in the light, with no stroke; the theme in octave violins; its continuation settles home
  (B–A–F♯–D), with no accent for the eagle.
- **The year after and the last pages:** a solo violin rises to the ninth. The last call is the intro's plain flute,
  with no cut, tap, turn or drone (the critics' M9), and distant horns answer.

**Effects that belong to one cut.** A: the bellows (a breath on every stroke) and the flint echo at the handover. B:
wind as the floor of the score, the dying ember's hiss, the lid of the fire-pot. C: the hearth, page turns, the pen's
scratch under each ink write-on, and a crackle whenever a page scorches.

# (9) The three biggest risks, and how to test them early

1. **A reads as unclear, or as a sermon.** The risk runs both ways, so the test does too. In week one, cut an animatic
   of A from the existing renders, with grey-box stand-ins for S4, S7 and S23–S25 (low-res marcher frames with light
   sprites), all the text in place and a temp score from the existing stems. Show it to 8–10 people outside the
   project and ask two questions:
   - "What happens on the mountain after the first fire?" At least seven should describe rivals coming out of the dark
     and carrying the fire together, slowly.
   - "Did any line feel like a message?" Any line three people flag gets cut, and only if the film then fails the
     first question does it come back rewritten.

   If the first answer fails, strengthen the setup in the picture (the keeper at the bellows, who sees only the wall),
   not with more text.
2. **The new showpieces miss the bar or cost too much.** Test the four riskiest shots first, each as one full-res key
   frame and a 48-frame half-res motion test judged at native resolution:
   - S24 THE CROSSING: composition, light trails, the star time-lapse;
   - S34 THE MAP RISES: the morph;
   - S29 THE VIGIL: the heroine in a new pose;
   - S23's tie-in: the figures.

   Price each one in CPU-hours from its test before booking cloud jobs. The fallbacks are decided now: S23 and S24
   work with lights alone; S34 can hide its whole middle under cloud; S29 can be a silhouette against the fire; S4 can
   shrink to the push into the core.
3. **C tips into fan fiction.** Set C's sixteen lines under rough versions of the ink pages (S32 as pencil tests) and
   read them with two Tolkien readers and two non-readers. Every reference must pass one question: does it move our
   story? Any reference that gets a knowing laugh instead of a nod goes. Settle two decisions with the producers in
   week one, because they define C: no Middle-earth names on screen, and the single quotation, *The last pages are
   for you.*
