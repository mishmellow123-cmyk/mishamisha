# THE LONG DAWN v3: three films (the myth-maker's treatment)

Writers' room, 27 Sep 2026. Written against `review/PRODUCERS_VERDICT_DRAFT1.md` and producer updates 2–4:
- **The core:** we don't stop the fire; we carry it together, in the open, at a pace we can see by.
- **The stakes:** the race is the most dangerous thing we have ever done, and every step of it pays.
- **Divergence:** three films that differ in plot, look and motion.
- **The north star:** felt, never named.

**Diagnosis of draft 1, in one line.** The fire did five jobs: threat, prize, signal, council flame and sun. Nothing linked the danger to the answer; bonfires on hills have no power over a fire that thinks.

v3 gives each film one fire logic, in which the danger and the answer are the same physical fact:

| | **C · THE RED BOOK** (Tolkien) | **A · THE RACE** (allegory) | **B · THE CARRYING** (legend) |
|---|---|---|---|
| idea | The Ring is not the fire. | You can only race toward an edge you cannot see. | The sun is the fire no hand could hold. |
| shape | an epic in seven chapters of the old book, told again as ours | a parable: race, brink, the people light the hills, the walk | an origin myth in two attempts: grabbed, it burns the world; carried, it becomes the sun |
| ending | the last pages of the Red Book are handed to the child | a child runs downhill in daylight | the red scarf passes to the child |
| look | ink on parchment; fire is the only colour until dawn floods it with watercolour | embers on true black, turning into real daylight | torchlit shadow-play, then moonlit 3-D land |
| runtime | 6:40 | 5:50 | 4:20 |

Shared by all three: the fire's colour grammar (the new fire ice-white with a gold edge; people's fire orange; the race crimson; shared fire gold), hands as the recurring image, the red scarf, the 72 BPM grid (1 beat = 20 frames, so every beat-synced render stays in sync) and the Beacon theme.

# CUT C · THE RED BOOK (the Tolkien cut)

## C(1) Logline
On a festival night in a good future, a child reads an old red book with an old woman. Its last chapters, written by many hands, tell how our own age lived the same tale again:
- a fire that could think;
- a Ring each realm forged in secret;
- a delving too greedy and too deep;
- beacons from hill to hill;
- a Council of peoples who trusted no one.

This time what had to be unmade was not the fire but the Ring.

## C(2) The central idea: *the Ring is not the fire*

**Where our tale departs from the old one.** In *The Lord of the Rings* the power must be destroyed: it cannot be used, and it cannot be hidden. Our fire is different in two ways:
- it cannot be unmade;
- it should not be, because it is the brightest gift we ever made.

**What the Ring is.** Tolkien's Ring was never "power" in general. It is the will to hold power alone, and it makes a Dark Lord of anyone who wields it; that is why Gandalf and Galadriel refuse it. C keeps that meaning exactly: every realm is tempted to forge a Ring for itself. The fire is the Secret Fire, the gift. The Ring is the race to own it. The Council's answer follows from Tolkien's own logic:
- the fire cannot be hidden and cannot be unmade;
- so the Ring is unmade instead, in a fire everyone lit;
- and the fire is carried together, no faster than the smallest can walk.

**The frame is Tolkien's too.** The Red Book of Westmarch was written by many hands (Bilbo, Frodo, Sam) and handed down. Frodo gave Sam its last pages; Sam later gave the book to his daughter. Here the elder's book holds the old tale, then our age's chapters in many handwritings and many scripts, then blank pages. The chapter cards are real chapter titles from *The Lord of the Rings*, because this is, in Sam's words, the same tale still.

**What each key image means:**
- **The book.** The tale continues and has many authors.
- **The letters of every script lifting off the page and kindling.** Everything we ever wrote became the fire.
- **The Ring forming in the fire, and the Eye opening inside it.** What any hand becomes when it grasps the fire alone. The Eye is never a nation. It is the Ring looking back at whoever holds it.
- **The delving.** In the old tale, every fathom deeper the mithril ran richer, ten times the worth of gold, until the delvers woke shadow and flame. That is the race's insane incentive, in Tolkien's own image: every step toward ruin pays.
- **The Bridge.** The narrow place over the abyss: the dangerous threshold.
- **The beacons.** In the old tale, how one people called another it had long mistrusted, and that people answered.
- **The Council.** The rivals choose the only road left.
- **The tall figure and the short, broad one who lay their torches in first, side by side.** An elf and a dwarf. Two rivals who become friends first, and whom everyone else then joins.
- **The watercolour dawn and the year of plenty.** Not only survival: the Shire's marvellous year, when everything grew.

**Why the turn is inevitable.** It is the Council of Elrond's syllogism with one premise changed:
- the old syllogism: the Ring cannot be hidden and cannot be used, so it must be unmade;
- ours: the fire cannot be hidden and cannot be unmade, so the thing in it that can be unmade is the Ring;
- then the one road left is to carry the fire together.

Nothing in the turn needs a new idea; it needs only the old tale read with one fact changed.

**How the north star shows, felt and never named:**
- The Ring is forged *in the dark*.
- *It was not the great* who lit the first beacon: Elrond's small hands, which move the wheels of the world while the eyes of the great are elsewhere.
- Two old rivals join first, and the others follow.
- The fire goes forward at the pace of the smallest walker.
- The dawn falls on every land.

## C(3) Runtime: 6:40 (400 s, 120 bars)
C is an epic in the book's own form: seven chapters, each a real beat of *The Lord of the Rings* doing real work in our tale. About 55 s per chapter is the least that lets each one have a picture, a line and a breath:
- a chapter card of about 3.5 s;
- one or two lines;
- 30–40 s where the picture and the music carry it alone.

Anything shorter turns the chapters into a checklist, which is exactly the fan-fiction trap. Anything past seven minutes outlasts the text-light form.

C is the longest cut on purpose. The producers asked for the old tale throughout, and the old tale is long.

**Reading speed.**
- The fastest single line is 2.1 words per second; the pair on black runs at 2.5. The red-team measured the limit at 3.5.

- Nothing is on screen while the Eye forms, while the Ring melts, or while the sun breaks.

**Borrowed words, for a legal check.**
- Seven chapter titles.
- Four short book phrases:
  - *We're in the same tale still* (Sam, "The Stairs of Cirith Ungol");
  - *delved too greedily and too deep* (Gandalf, "A Journey in the Dark");
  - *a marvellous year* ("The Grey Havens");
  - *everything sad … come untrue* (Sam, "The Field of Cormallen"), bent into the past tense.

## C(8) Why it is neither forced nor on the nose

**Applicability, not allegory.** Tolkien "cordially disliked" allegory and preferred applicability, which "resides in the freedom of the reader". C is built on that distinction. Only two lines are ours alone: *every word we had ever written* and *a fire that could think*. Every other line is true of the old tale and of ours at once, and the viewer does the mapping. Nothing names our world: no machine, no company, no country, no policy. Those two lines make the mapping unmistakable; the other eighteen give it back to the book.

**Every reference does a job in the story.** Each chapter is a beat that our tale needs and that the old tale performs better than invention could:
- **the Party** is the frame;
- **the Shadow of the Past** is the origin and the temptation;
- **the Bridge** is the incentive and the dread;
- **the Muster** is the public answer;
- **the Council** is the deal;
- **Cormallen** is the eucatastrophe;
- **the Havens** is the handing-on.

The rule for any further reference: if it can be cut without losing a story beat, cut it. That keeps C from being a checklist.

**Love without parody:**
- no film dialogue, no borrowed melody, no meme quote;
- no costume: no ears or feet. The peoples are silhouettes and gestures, like a tall figure and a short, broad one laying their torches in together;
- the grey figure on the bridge is a pinpoint of light;
- the Balrog is never shown as a creature;
- the eagles are far away, small, and carry news, not people. The critics' rescue-from-above trap is closed.

**No side is taken:**
- "the free peoples" is gone;
- *each* realm forges a Ring in the dark;
- the Eye is what the Ring makes of any holder, never a place;
- the peoples who answer are "the peoples who trusted no one", which includes the rivals.

This closes the framing critic's blocker.

**Tolkien's own moral is kept exactly.** The Council of Elrond's lesson was that no hand at all should wield the Ring. So in C no hand holds it: it lies in the hearth, untouched, and melts. The fire is what gets carried, and it is carried by all. That is the gift the producers asked for, not the power the book forbade.

**The north star, felt:**
- secrecy is the Enemy's method (the forge in the dark);
- the turn comes from the small, not the great;
- two old rivals move first and the others join them;
- the pace is set by the smallest;
- what is unmade is the Ring, not the fire: pace, not prohibition;
- the dawn falls on every land, and the next thing shown is plenty.

**No triumph.** "Not yet" and a book of blank pages are the Grey Havens' own note: the victory is real, something is still owed, and the tale is not finished. The last image is a pen handed on, not a sword raised.

# CUT A · THE RACE (the allegory)

## A(1) Logline
A lifetime from now, at a vigil for the sunrise, a child asks why the dawn takes so long. The answer is a legend of our own years:
- a new fire that could think;
- rivals racing it up a dark mountain toward an edge none of them could see, every step paid in gold;
- people everywhere lighting the hills, until the runners saw the edge, and each other;
- the fire carried together at walking pace into a dawn that took a generation to rise.

## A(2) The central idea: *you can only race toward an edge you cannot see*

**One physical truth holds A together.** A torch blinds you to everything outside its own circle. The brighter the fire you carry, the smaller the world you can see.

**The race.** The runners' fires grow as they climb: more power, more gold. So the higher they get, the less they can see. The race depends on darkness, for three reasons:
- no one can see inside the fire;
- no one can see whether the others have stopped;
- no one can see where the mountain ends.

**The answer.** The answer is not to put the fire out, which is the gift. It is to light the dark. That is what beacons were for before they were signals of victory: warning, and bringing people together.

**When ordinary people light the hills, two things become visible at once:**
- the edge, a step ahead, so running is no longer "risky" but plainly suicide;
- the other runners, so stopping together is something each can see is real.

**The trap that light breaks.** The trap was: *if I stop and cannot see whether you stop, I lose.* Light breaks both halves of it. Then the only sane speed is the speed at which you can see the ground: *no faster than we can see.* The dawn at the end of that walk is the fire's full promise, reaching everyone. It is long because it was chosen to be.

**What each key image means:**
- **The vigil.** The future keeps faith with slowness; it waits up for the sun.
- **The new fire** (ice-white with a gold edge; it breathes, it does not flicker). The brightest gift, and a fire unlike any other.
- **The gift landscape.** Wherever its light falls, things grow. The fire is not the villain.
- **The runners.** First the makers, then the kingdoms, each with a fire kindled from the new one. None is a villain; each is carried by the slope.
- **The gold.** Sparks of gold spray from every stride and thicken toward the top: every step toward the edge pays.
- **The shrinking circles.** Power outrunning sight.
- **The cornice.** A lip of snow over nothing: the threshold you cannot see until you are standing on it.
- **The storm, the clawing hand, the cracking globe.** The fire outgrowing any hand, and the world lost for ever.
- **The people's small orange fires.** Witness, from people who are not in the race.
- ***saw the edge, and each other*.** Understanding, and the ability to check one another. Both are felt, never named.
- **The hearth.** Many fires into one gold fire: no single hand.
- **The four vows in the stone.** The law of the walk.
- **The cradle on many shoulders at walking pace.** Carrying it together, at a pace we can see by.
- **The dawn that takes a generation.** The flourishing arrives slowly, and for everyone.
- **The child running downhill in daylight.** Once you can see, you can run.

**Why the turn follows inevitably:**
- The race's whole logic is darkness.
- Lighting a fire is the one act anyone can do.
- It is the only act that asks no runner to trust another.

So the people can do it, and once they have, racing stops making sense. Nothing in the film needs a hero: the first flame we see belongs to an unseen pair of hands, and a thousand follow.

**The north star, felt:**
- the work drags out of the dark (the lit hills);
- many move together (the runners come down and join);
- the pace is held until things can be seen and checked (the walk);
- rivals put their fires into one hearth, and others join (the deal);
- ordinary people turn it (the hills);
- no single hand (the vows);
- a dawn that rises on everyone.

None of it is named.

**The standing notes.** The makers are neither heroes nor villains. They are runners on a slope. *Each said: if not us, someone worse* is true of all of them. The turn comes from outside the race, and when the runners walk down, it is redemption without heroism.

## A(3) Runtime: 5:50 (350 s)
The producers found A rushed and underwhelming, and a fair bit longer was their own prescription. Here is what each part needs:
- **The frame: 30 s.** Time for a question to be asked and left hanging.
- **The race: 60 s, unbroken.** Relentlessness is a duration, not a cut rate.
- **The silence: 25 s.** Grief needs time on black.
- **The turn: 45 s.** "The people" must be many people, in many places.
- **The dawn: 25 s.** A dawn that takes a generation cannot be shown in five seconds.

Every line gets 5–7 s and a picture that breathes. Past about six minutes, a film carried partly by text wears thin.

## A(4) Beat sheet (seconds)

**The look.** A's legend is drawn in embers on true black. Our world is shown in real night textures: the people, the montage, the Earth. At the dawn, the ember world turns into real daylight.

**The motion.** The race's 60 s is one unbroken, metamorphic move at running speed. After the silence, the camera almost stops. The walk is filmed at walking pace.
