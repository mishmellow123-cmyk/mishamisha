# THE LONG DAWN v3: three films (the myth-maker's treatment)

Writers' room, 27 Sep 2026. Written against `review/PRODUCERS_VERDICT_DRAFT1.md` and producer updates 2–4:
- **The core:** we don't stop the fire; we carry it together, in the open, at a pace we can see by.
- **The stakes:** the race is the most dangerous thing we have ever done, and every step of it pays.
- **Divergence:** three films that differ in plot, look and motion.
- **The north star:** felt, never named.

**Diagnosis of draft 1, in one line.** The fire did five jobs: threat, prize, signal, council flame and sun. Nothing linked the danger to the answer; bonfires on hills have no power over a fire that thinks.

v3 gives each film one fire logic, in which the danger and the answer are the same physical fact:

| | **C · THE RED BOOK** (Tolkien) | **A · THE RACE** (allegory) | **B · THE CARRYING** (legend) |
|---|---|---|---|
| idea | The Ring is not the fire. | You can only race toward an edge you cannot see. | The sun is the fire no hand could hold. |

# CUT C · THE RED BOOK (the Tolkien cut)

## C(1) Logline
On a festival night in a good future, a child reads an old red book with an old woman. Its last chapters, written by many hands, tell how our own age lived the same tale again:
- a fire that could think;
- a Ring each realm forged in secret;
- a delving too greedy and too deep;
- beacons from hill to hill;
- a Council of peoples who trusted no one.

This time what had to be unmade was not the fire but the Ring.

## C(2) The central idea: *the Ring is not the fire*

**Where our tale departs from the old one.** In *The Lord of the Rings* the power must be destroyed: it cannot be used, and it cannot be hidden. Our fire is different in two ways:
- it cannot be unmade;
- it should not be, because it is the brightest gift we ever made.

**What the Ring is.** Tolkien's Ring was never "power" in general. It is the will to hold power alone, and it makes a Dark Lord of anyone who wields it; that is why Gandalf and Galadriel refuse it. C keeps that meaning exactly: every realm is tempted to forge a Ring for itself. The fire is the Secret Fire, the gift. The Ring is the race to own it. The Council's answer follows from Tolkien's own logic:
- the fire cannot be hidden and cannot be unmade;
- so the Ring is unmade instead, in a fire everyone lit;
- and the fire is carried together, no faster than the smallest can walk.

**The frame is Tolkien's too.** The Red Book of Westmarch was written by many hands (Bilbo, Frodo, Sam) and handed down. Frodo gave Sam its last pages; Sam later gave the book to his daughter. Here the elder's book holds the old tale, then our age's chapters in many handwritings and many scripts, then blank pages. The chapter cards are real chapter titles from *The Lord of the Rings*, because this is, in Sam's words, the same tale still.

**What each key image means:**
- **The book.** The tale continues and has many authors.
- **The letters of every script lifting off the page and kindling.** Everything we ever wrote became the fire.
- **The Ring forming in the fire, and the Eye opening inside it.** What any hand becomes when it grasps the fire alone. The Eye is never a nation. It is the Ring looking back at whoever holds it.
- **The delving.** In the old tale, every fathom deeper the mithril ran richer, ten times the worth of gold, until the delvers woke shadow and flame. That is the race's insane incentive, in Tolkien's own image: every step toward ruin pays.
- **The Bridge.** The narrow place over the abyss: the dangerous threshold.
- **The beacons.** In the old tale, how one people called another it had long mistrusted, and that people answered.
- **The Council.** The rivals choose the only road left.
- **The tall figure and the short, broad one who lay their torches in first, side by side.** An elf and a dwarf. Two rivals who become friends first, and whom everyone else then joins.
- **The watercolour dawn and the year of plenty.** Not only survival: the Shire's marvellous year, when everything grew.

**Why the turn is inevitable.** It is the Council of Elrond's syllogism with one premise changed:
- the old syllogism: the Ring cannot be hidden and cannot be used, so it must be unmade;
- ours: the fire cannot be hidden and cannot be unmade, so the thing in it that can be unmade is the Ring;
- then the one road left is to carry the fire together.

Nothing in the turn needs a new idea; it needs only the old tale read with one fact changed.

**How the north star shows, felt and never named:**
- The Ring is forged *in the dark*.
- *It was not the great* who lit the first beacon: Elrond's small hands, which move the wheels of the world while the eyes of the great are elsewhere.
- Two old rivals join first, and the others follow.
- The fire goes forward at the pace of the smallest walker.
- The dawn falls on every land.

## C(3) Runtime: 6:40 (400 s, 120 bars)
C is an epic in the book's own form: seven chapters, each a real beat of *The Lord of the Rings* doing real work in our tale. About 55 s per chapter is the least that lets each one have a picture, a line and a breath:
- a chapter card of about 3.5 s;
- one or two lines;
- 30–40 s where the picture and the music carry it alone.

Anything shorter turns the chapters into a checklist, which is exactly the fan-fiction trap. Anything past seven minutes outlasts the text-light form.

C is the longest cut on purpose. The producers asked for the old tale throughout, and the old tale is long.
