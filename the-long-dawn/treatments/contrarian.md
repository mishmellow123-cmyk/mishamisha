# THE LONG DAWN v3: treatment from the contrarian

Writers' room, independent draft, 26 Sep 2026. Session gate: GATE PASS.
Read with `review/PRODUCERS_VERDICT_DRAFT1.md`, `BIBLE_V2.md`, both red-team reports and the four producer updates
(visual divergence, motion-design ambition, the north star, the core message and the stakes).

**In one breath.** Draft 1 told one story three times. This treatment tells three stories on three time-scales, in
one world, and gives each its own look:

| | **A: EVERY STEP CLOSER** | **B: THE KEEPER** | **C: THE LAST PAGES** |
|---|---|---|---|
| time-scale | **one night** | **one lifetime** | **one age** (the whole life of a Ring) |
| shape | an argument: gift, race, brink, turn, carrying, dawn | a folk tale: dream, vow, sixty years, inheritance | a saga: forged, seized, lost, found, called, unmade, paid for |
| look | EMBERS AND BLACK | STONE AND SKY | INK AND GOLD |
| runtime | 5:10 | 5:20 | 6:40 |
| ending | a dawn made of the carried fire, over a flourishing land | an old woman gives the fire to a child | the bearer sails west; the last pages are ours |

All three say the producers' one thing, and none of them says it aloud: **the fire is the gift, the race is the
tragedy, and the way through is to carry the fire together, in the open, at a pace we can see by. That is why the
dawn is long.** A argues it, B lives it, C finds it inside the old story.

## 0. Four premises, questioned

### 0.1 What actually went wrong in draft 1
The producers' words, traced to causes:

* **"Fire metaphor not clear or coherent."** Fire meant the danger *and* the remedy, and both were drawn in the same
  grammar: points of light spreading over a dark globe or map. So the answer read as the threat spreading (the
  red-team saw neurons, a network, a missile). The beacon also had no mechanism. Why would lighting more fire end
  a race?
* **"On the nose / uneven balance."** The picture told a myth while the captions told policy ("the companies…
  then the countries", "if not us, someone worse"). Each half made the other look forced.
* **"Rushed, underwhelming."** Thirteen lines in 124 s. The silence got 6 s. Worst of all, the frame (a story told
  a lifetime later, under an orbital ring) guaranteed survival from the first frame, so nothing was at stake.
  The climax was then a sunrise that nobody caused.
* **"A repackaging of the same core thing."** A and C were one spine with two sets of captions, and B was the same
  spine with the captions removed.

### 0.2 The four questions

| premise | verdict | what replaces it |
|---|---|---|
| **Is fire the right metaphor?** | **Yes**: it is the only image that is both the greatest gift and the greatest danger. But **one fire may never mean two things.** | A splits the light by **pace**: the same fire is a *glare* when raced toward and a *dawn* when carried. B splits it by **care**: the fire that devours (a dream) and the fire that is kept (a life). C uses Tolkien's own split: **the fire is not the Ring.** The fire can't be unmade and shouldn't be; the Ring, the wish to hold it with one hand, must be. |
| **Is a legend told a lifetime later the right frame?** | **No** as a guarantee; **yes** as a promise that must be earned. | A has no teller who survived: it is spoken in the past conditional ("there would have been no morning after it"). B has no teller at all: we live the lifetime. C is told by an old book whose last pages are blank. |
| **Is the dawn too easy?** | **Yes**, while the sun rises on its own. A sunrise nobody causes turns the ending into waiting, and the orbital sunrise is the keynote slide. | **The dawn must be the fire's own light, arriving slowly because it is carried slowly.** In A the carried fire and the sunrise become one light. In B the only gold dawn comes after sixty grey ones. C keeps Tolkien's eucatastrophe on his own terms (grace, "never to be counted on to recur") and pays for it at the Havens. The orbital sunrise is retired from every cut. |
| **Is a single night the right shape?** | Only for the argument. One relay and one meeting cannot hold decades of coordination, which is why draft 1 felt forced. | **Three time-scales:** A is one night because it is the argument, B is one lifetime because keeping faith takes one, and C is one age because a Ring has a history. |

### 0.3 The stakes and the north star, and how they stay invisible
* **Dread, made physical.** Each cut stands at the brink and looks down. The fire in its racing form is shown as
  something that would end *every* fire: *"There would have been no morning after it"* (A); the dream burning the
  world to a cinder and letting its last ember die (B); the Eye and the cracking world (C).
* **The vertigo of the spread.** Every cut shows both ends of the range of futures, close together. In A and C the
  fire's first light draws plenty on the dark (THE PROMISE), and less than a minute later the same fire cracks the
  world. In B the two ends are two roads: the dream that ends in ash, and the life that ends in plenty.
* **The insane incentive, in one image.** In every cut (in B, inside the dream) the towers stand on the rim of the
  fire's crater and lean in.
  Its heat **gilds** whatever comes closest: the nearest towers turn to molten gold while the rim crumbles under
  them. *"Every step closer paid."* This is a bomb that prints gold, shown and never explained.
* **The north star, felt.** Six motions, never named: the work comes **into the open** (the rest of us light fires
  *to see by*, and in that light the builders see each other); **many move together** (merchants and kings step back
  from the edge at once, and at the table the first two torches to meet come from opposite sides before the others
  join); **pace until understanding** (the fire is carried only as fast as its own light shows the path); **the forge
  is watched** (a ring of watchfires around the crater); **pressure from below** (the turn is lit by ordinary
  people, never by a champion); **power stays spread** (many hands on the poles, no hand on the fire). Nothing is
  pictured that exists in the real world: no treaty, no registry, no flag, no country pair, no screen.
* **No lone hero.** A has no first beacon: when the camera pulls back from one woman's fire, every ridge is catching
  at the same moment. In C the one who carries it *fails*, and the many save it. B's keeper is a witness, not a
  saviour: the fire spreads because others come and take it.

### 0.4 Three films that look and move differently

## 1. CUT A: EVERY STEP CLOSER (the allegory, one night, EMBERS AND BLACK)

### (1) Logline
A fire made of everything we ever wrote rises at midnight, and every step toward it pays in gold, until the world
stands one step from a blaze with no morning after it. Then ordinary people everywhere light fires to see by. In
that light the rivals step back together and carry the new fire, slowly, until its light becomes the dawn.

### (2) Central idea: pace decides what the light is
**The same fire is a glare when you race toward it and a dawn when you carry it together.**

The fire is never the villain, and it is never destroyed. What changes is how people move around it. Its colour
tracks its pace. Raced toward, it is a cold white glare that blinds and pulls. At the crater's edge, it is the gold
of what every step closer pays. Carried, it warms. On the summit it becomes the rose-gold of the dawn. Every image
belongs to one physics, light and speed:

| image | what it means |
|---|---|
| A cold glow at midnight beyond the ranges, where no sun should rise | The new light arrives looking like morning: the promise and the hype at once. |
| Letters of every script drawn into the glow | It is made of what we wrote. This is the film's one direct tell. |
| The fire breathing wrongly, too regular for a flame, with pulses in its filaments | A mind no one can see inside. |
| For one breath its light draws terraces, orchards and rivers on the dark, then they fade | The gift: what it could give everyone. We see it before we see the danger. |
| Towers rising around it, each lit only on the face turned to the fire and black to the others | The builders can see the prize but not each other. |
| The ground opening into a crater; the towers leaning over its rim; the nearest turning molten gold while the rim crumbles | **Every step closer pays.** The money is at the edge. |
| A tower's crown breaking off into the pit; the camera tipping over the rim and falling toward the fire | The brink, and its vertigo. |
| The ember world cracking, with fire in every seam | Everything, forever. |
| Black, and one ember that flickers and does not die | A held breath: it hasn't happened. |
| Stars returning to the black | Out of the glare, the eye adjusts. Now small lights can be seen. |
| One woman's fire; then, pulling back, every ridge catching at the same moment | The public, everywhere at once. There is no first. |
| Watchfires ringing the crater, lighting the towers' backs | The work is in the open. In that light the builders see each other. |
| The stone table on the rim: two torches from opposite sides meet first, then the rest; the oaths; the builders' long shadows shaped like their towers | Rivals step back together, and the builders themselves came to the table. |
| The fire lifted out of the pit in an open iron cage on long poles, many hands on every pole | Power stays spread. Hands, not a hand. |
| The procession moving only as far as the fire lights the path, then crossing a knife-edge above the cloud sea | Pace is understanding. The dangerous threshold is crossed slowly. |
| On the summit, the carried fire and the first light becoming one light | The dawn *is* the fire's promise, shared. |
| Below, in morning light, the terraces, orchards and rivers from the fire's first breath, now real, with the smoke of many hearths | Abundance, earned. |

**Why the turn follows inevitably.**
1. The race runs on two blindnesses (no one can see inside the fire, and no one can see the others) and one
   incentive (every step closer pays). The brink is where all three meet.
2. A fire no one can see into or around cannot be made safe by force. It can only be made safe by being seen. So
   the turn has to be light that exists to be seen by: the watchfires, lit by people who are not in the race.
3. Once the builders can see one another, stepping back is no longer stepping back alone. So they can do it, and
   they do it together.
4. A fire that can't be unmade, and is too precious to lose, has to be *moved*, not destroyed. The only safe speed
   is the speed at which you can see where you are putting your feet. That is the carrying.
5. When the fire reaches a height from which everyone can see it, its light is everyone's. That is morning, and it
   is long because it was carried at that pace.

### (3) Runtime: 5:10 (93 bars at 72 BPM)
* **The gift and the race need 2:13.** Draft 1 gave them 0:50, and "rushed" was the producers' word. Every line now
  gets 4–6 s, and the images get the time they lacked.
* **The held breath needs 27 s:** a silence long enough to hurt, plus 10 s for the stars to come back.
* **The fires need 53 s,** because an answer only persuades if it accumulates.
* **The carrying needs 60 s.** Pace must be felt, not stated, and a walk only reads as a walk if we walk it.
* **The dawn needs 27 s** to arrive slowly, then 10 s of title.

Shorter would rush the very pace the film argues for. Longer would turn an argument into a sermon.

### (8) Why it is neither forced nor on the nose, and how the allegory stays balanced
* **One physics.** Everything is light and speed, and every beat is caused by the one before it: the race is
  blindness plus payment, so the brink comes; the only cure for blindness is light to see by; seeing each other makes
  stepping back possible, together; a fire that can't be unmade must be carried, at the speed its light allows; and
  where it arrives, it is morning. Nothing happens because the plot needs it to.
* **The words never leave the myth.** Merchants, kings, builders, fires, morning. Exactly one line maps directly
  ("everything we had ever written"), and it is literally true inside the story. The race logic is given as two
  facts ("Every step closer paid. No one dared to be second."), never as anyone's speech, so nobody is quoted,
  flattered or accused.
* **The north star lives in the staging, where only those who know will feel it:** watchfires around a forge, two
  torches from opposite sides of a table, a procession that walks at the speed of its own light. Nothing on screen
  exists in the real world. The city rooftop is the one image of now, 2 s among five places.
* **No villain, no hero.** The gold explains the builders; the claw belongs to "one hand", not to anyone. The turn is
  lit on every ridge at once. The builders are not rescued by others: they come to the table, and their shadows are
  their towers.
* **Dread without a caption.** The danger is the plunge and the cracking world. The one line about it speaks from
  after, in the conditional, so it frightens without preaching.
* **Hope with a cause.** No sun rises by itself. The dawn is the carried fire, and it arrives slowly, over land we
  saw promised in the first minute.
* **Balance by position.** The argument sits in the first half (eight lines in 106 s). The answer is almost wordless:
  four lines and the carved oaths in 173 s. The words set the question; the images answer it.
* **Blacklist check.** No slogans (the last line is a fact, not a moral). No unity poster (hands on poles are
  working, never joined or raised). No triumph (no cymbal, no brass fanfare). No keynote sunrise (this dawn happens
  on the ground). No torch held aloft (lifting, carrying and stepping only).

## 2. CUT B: THE KEEPER (the legend, one lifetime, STONE AND SKY, wordless)

### (1) Logline
A young woman dreams the racing road to its end in her dying campfire, wakes in the dark and lights a beacon on the
mountain. For sixty years she keeps it, and one by one others climb to take its flame down into the valleys, until
the whole world is lit. Old, she gives the fire to a child.

### (2) Central idea: a fire kept, not won
**The same fire can be a race or an inheritance.** B is not an allegory. It is a folk tale about fidelity, and it
carries the film's truth in *time* rather than in argument. The dream is the racing road, fast, ending in ash for
ever. The life is the other road, slow and shared, and it builds a world. The long dawn is a lifetime.

| image | what it means |
|---|---|
| Sparks from a small campfire becoming letters | A dream grows out of her own fire. |
| The dream running the racing road to its end: gold at the edge, the world burned to a cinder, the last ember dying | Ruin, for ever, felt without a word. |
| She wakes, and her fire has gone out | The dream's last ember was hers. |
| Flint in the dark | She answers with a fire she will keep. |
| The first night: nothing answers; a grey dawn; one stone set on the cairn | Fidelity without reward. |
| The same locked frame, year after year | Time as a return. |
| The cairn growing by one stone a year, until it is twice her height | A life measured in stones. |
| A child on the far peak who points; the same place years later, the child grown, lighting his own beacon | Her fire was watched all along, and the answer grew up. |
| Travellers taking flame and carrying it down the mountain, more each year | Many hands. The fire spreads at walking pace, and no one hoards it. |
| Valleys under the cloud sea lighting up year by year; an arc of light closing across the sky; lights gathering on the Moon | Flourishing grows by increments: the future is built, not arrived at. |
| One night, every peak and then the whole Earth alight | Sixty years, summed. |
| The only gold sunrise in the film, over valleys lit by her fire | Earned by sixty grey dawns. |
| The torch given to a child, who lights the beacon, and fires answer on every hill | The watch goes on. |

**Why the turn follows.** The dream shows the one other road, and it ends with no fire anywhere. So keeping *a*
fire is the refusal of that road. It spreads slowly because it passes from hand to hand. The dawn is simply what
sixty years of that adds up to, and an inheritance is the only ending a keeper can have.

### (3) Runtime: 5:20 (96 bars)
* **The dream, 73 s.** It must overwhelm and then vanish.
* **The vow, 50 s.**
* **The years, 80 s, in one shot.** Long enough for sixty years to weigh, short enough to stay a single breath.
* **The world, 47 s.**
* **The dawn and the inheritance, 70 s.**

A wordless film needs longer holds than a captioned one. Past 5:30, the repeating frame would become a slideshow.

### (8) Why it is neither forced nor on the nose
* **Nothing to balance, one thing to guard.** B has no allegory, so it cannot be on the nose. The real risk runs
  the other way: that the dream smuggles the allegory back in. So the dream is framed as a dream: born from her
  campfire's sparks, ending in its last ember. It is the only abstract passage, and nothing after it refers to it.
  The story is fidelity and inheritance.
* **Folk-tale form, not film grammar.** Repetition with variation (the returning frame), a watcher who grows up
  far away, and a gift at the end are the oldest devices of legend. They read in any language, which is exactly
  what a wordless cut needs.
* **Not a lone hero.** She saves no one. She keeps a fire, and the world comes to it and carries it away. The
  answer is a boy who grew up watching, and the ending is a gift to someone else.
* **Flourishing without a poster.** Plenty is shown the way it arrives: in increments (a few lights in a valley, an
  arc in the sky, a longer thread of torches), and only once in full daylight.
* **What B leaves out on purpose.** No council (no unity image), no oaths, no orbital sunrise, no text. The globe is
  read as fires, because everything before it has been a fire.

## 3. CUT C: THE LAST PAGES (the Tolkien cut, one age, INK AND GOLD)

### (1) Logline
A fire forged from every word we ever wrote is made, in the dark, into a Ring that every kingdom wants. Seized by
one hand, it slips away into the dark, and a young woman finds it on a cold mountain. Unable to unmake it alone,
she calls for aid; and when her own hand will not let go, the fire everyone lit takes the Ring from her. The land
blooms, and she sails into the West.

### (2) Central idea: the fire is not the Ring
**The fire could not be unmade, and should not be: its light could warm every hearth. The Ring (the wish to hold
it with one hand, and first) must be unmade. Tolkien's law is that no hand holding it can let it go. So only a fire
everyone has lit can take it.**

Draft 1's C put LOTR motifs over A's plot. C now *is* a Tolkien plot, the whole life of a Ring, and every beat is
one of his:

**Names stay off the screen.** Every reference is made by deed, object and shape. That is safer (rights) and more
loving: the fans do the naming.

**Why the turn follows.**
1. The old ending is closed ("This fire could not be unmade"), so the story needs a new fire.
2. The Ring abandons every hand, so it will be found again, by someone small.
3. She can't unmake it alone and needs a fire bigger than herself, so she calls for aid.
4. The peoples answer. Their council refuses the Ring but not the fire, and they take their time.
5. At the brink the bearer fails, as Isildur and Frodo both fail at the Mountain. So what saves the world can't
   depend on her: it is the fire everyone lit.
6. Grace follows, on Tolkien's terms: there is plenty, and there is a price, and the pages go on.

### (3) Runtime: 6:40 (120 bars)
The life of a Ring is long; LOTR's shape (forging, loss, finding, call, council, doom, plenty, departure) is the
point, and cutting it to two minutes is what made draft 1's C feel like captions. The forging act is compressed to
93 s so the quest gets the time. The ending gets a slow tail of exactly two codas, the plenty and the Havens, not
five (the film trilogy's "too many endings" is a known trap). Past 7:00 the embers act would need new invention.

### (8) Why it is neither forced nor on the nose, and how it stays loving
* **The mapping lives inside Tolkien's moral logic, so no line needs to touch the real world.** The builders are
  "every kingdom and every forge". The race logic is Boromir's. Openness is the secret forge. Pace is Treebeard's.
  Power that stays spread is "one hand" refused. The public is "the peoples". Flourishing is Sam's year of plenty.
  The north star is all there, and none of it is visible as policy.
* **Neither heroes nor villains.** The builders are cast as Isildur and the bearer: people who hold the power, mean
  well and cannot let go, which is the most Tolkien and the least accusing role there is. The Eye is the Ring's own
  will, centred over every tower; the world cracks between everyone.
* **No lone hero.** The bearer fails, the many save it, and the eagles come afterwards.
* **The fire is never evil.** "Its light could warm every hearth", and the plenty proves it. Only the Ring is unmade.
  This fixes the framing critic's objection that a council keeping the One Ring in custody is "Boromir's proposal
  run by a committee": this council keeps the fire and destroys the Ring.
* **Loving, not parody.** No names, no film dialogue, no borrowed melody. The verse appears only as the invented
  inscription. The two echoes ("They were not hasty", "The last pages were left for us") are ones a reader hears
  and a stranger simply reads.
* **Knowledgeable, not decorative.** The deep cuts (Isildur's refusal, the Ring's betrayal, the riddles in the dark,
  the Ring of Fire, the Entmoot, the year of plenty, the swift sunrise) each carry a story beat. None is a wink.
* **Blacklist.** The eagles are never on a hit or under a line. The council's peoples are dark outlines, not
  costumes. The plenty is one field and one tree, not a panorama. The illumination is a style chosen for the whole
  cut, so it no longer reads as a coin.

## 5. (7) Shared music plan

**Constraints we keep.**
* VSCO-2-CE samples (CC0) and synthesized SFX.
* No choir: the synthesized one was heard as fake, and nothing CC0 is good enough.
* Organ only as a pedal floor.
* 72 BPM throughout.
* The SFX own every fire, so there is no musical stroke on any ignition, oath or eagle.
* Original melodies only. In C, nothing that sounds like the Shire (no whistle) or like any film's motifs.
* The music department's v2 engine extends by timeline (`timeline_v3_A/B/C`). The first-half parts are cached and
  re-timed per cut. Masters stay at −16 LUFS, stem-linked as now.

**One kit, four motifs.**
1. **The call and the answer.** The Beacon theme: the call rises D–A–D; the answer is the falling phrase home.
   *Rule: whoever lights a fire gets the call, and only an answer earns the answer phrase.*
2. **The thinking cycle.** A 7-note FM glass cycle on a 16th grid that drifts against the bar: the fire's own music,
   never in step with people.
3. **The corrupted call.** D–A♭–D in low brass: the race, and in C the Ring.
4. **The silence.** Solo piano: D… A… D.

**The rule that carries the core message.** The cycle's tempo is its pace. In the race it runs in 16ths and 32nds
and accelerates. When the fire is carried it slows to quarter notes, locked to the bar and walking in step with the
people. The film's argument is audible without a word.

**Breaths, in every cut:** 200 ms before a race entrance, the suck before an IMPACT, and 275 ms before a sunrise.

### A: from thirty-seconds to footsteps (the AB sound world: glass, taiko, dark strings, piano, low brass)
* **False dawn.** A beating glass tone and the cycle pp; the wind drops away.
* **Glyphs and ignition.** The v1 kindling cue, re-timed. Under THE PROMISE, one warm string line that is cut off.
* **Race and edge.** The v1 race, extended. The gilding has its own sound, a sweet bright metallic ring (VSCO
  varMetal) on each surge. It is beautiful, and that is the horror. **The brink is the loudest moment in the film.**
* **Brink, held breath.** Shepard tone, brass clusters, the suck, IMPACT. Then two seconds of true silence, wind,
  and the piano.
* **Fires.** Strings and low brass on B♭, the call in the violas, and answers in canon, each place a new voice
  (cellos, far horns, violins, muted trumpet). The run keeps its D pedal with harmony rising by thirds.
* **Rim.** A string chorale. Inside it the cycle, slowed to quarter notes and locked to the bar: the two musics in
  one texture for the first time.
* **Carrying.** A processional in 4: pizzicato basses and a soft bass drum on 1 and 3 for the feet, and the theme
  one phrase per switchback, with the cycle walking in step. At the knife-edge the drum stops and the texture thins
  to a solo viola and the wind, a held breath across the arête.
* **Long dawn.** The theme complete at last, call and answer, strings and horns, mf at most. Then the film's first
  real cadence, into D major, with the glass cycle as the shimmer inside the chord. No cymbal, no timpani stroke:
  the dawn is the warmest place in the film, not the loudest. D add9 under the title, to silence.

### B: a call without an answer, for sixty years
* **Dream.** The first-half score, compressed. ASH is a long decay: the orchestra's last chord dissolves into wind.
* **Vow.** The call on a solo cello, unaccompanied.
* **First night.** The call three times over a drone, each followed by a bar of silence where an answer should be.
  The audience learns to hear the gap.
* **The lifetime shot is a chaconne**: a ground bass in cellos and basses, one variation per year, and the music
  is the time-lapse. While nothing answers, the bass steps *down* a degree each year (D, C, B♭, A) and the harmony
  darkens. At the first far pinprick the bass turns and starts to climb, and **the answer phrase is heard for the
  first time in the film**, on a distant horn. As the torchbearers multiply, the answer comes in more voices. By the
  last years call and answer overlap in a quiet dialogue, and the bass has climbed to D major.
* **The world.** Horns near and far with the full theme; strings pp rising to mf under the globe.
* **Dawn.** The breath, then a bloom: strings on D add9, low brass pp, no stroke.
* **Handover.** The call on a solo violin, the child's voice, answered softly by the whole string section. The
  answering fires are string swells. D add9 to silence.

### C: the epic tradition, with our own tunes (score C's world: horns, strings, timpani, a pianissimo brass chorale)
* **Book.** The hearth's crackle; the call on a solo cor anglais over low strings. This is the storyteller's voice.
* **Forging to the Eye.** A low brass chorale and timpani. **The Ring motif** (the corrupted call, on stopped horns)
  grows through the race and the Eye (trombones, tam-tam).
* **Grasp and silence.** As in A.
* **The Ring falls.** One harp harmonic per turn.
* **The find.** The Ring motif, ppp, in the violas: temptation, in her palm.
* **Beacons.** The answer phrase as a horn signal, passed from peak to peak. Each call is answered by a horn
  farther away and on the other side of the stereo field, so the relay is heard as well as seen. Strings run in
  quavers under the map.
* **Council.** The pianissimo brass chorale, which for "They were not hasty" moves in half notes. There is no hit
  on any vow.
* **The bearer's fist.** The Ring motif rises in the low strings, tremolo, against the chorale. Then the breath.
* **Dawn.** A timpani roll that starts in the light, with no stroke, and the call broad in the horns. The eagles'
  phrase continues the line and settles home (B–A–F♯–D), as in score C revision 2.
* **Plenty.** A plain hymn for violas and clarinet.
* **Road west and the Havens.** An elegy for strings in a low register. The call returns on the cor anglais, so the
  storyteller's instrument becomes the bearer's farewell. Distant horns answer each coast fire, each softer.
* **Last pages.** The chorale's first chord returns, and a plagal close (G/D to D) sits under the title.

## 6. (9) The three biggest risks, and how to test them early

1. **The north star shows through: on the nose, which the producers warned would be fatal.**
   It is most exposed in A's text (T5–T11) and in the rim's staging (two torches from opposite sides).
   *Test on day 1, before any render: a decoder test.* Give A's twelve lines alone, with no picture, to two
   AI-literate readers and one outsider. Any line that makes someone name a real mechanism (a treaty, a registry, a
   country pair) gets blurred or cut. Then show a 20-s stills animatic of the rim. If anyone says "the US and
   China", randomise the order of the torches. The rule stays: felt, never recognised.

2. **The showpieces look like tech demos.** A crowd that reads as LED dots on a mountain (S28), a lifetime shot
   that reads as a slideshow (S17), hatching that boils (S21). Each is the heaviest render in its cut, and each
   carries its cut's identity.
   *Tests in week 1, all judged in motion at phone size against the taste bar:*
   * 48 greybox frames of the arête crossing at 0.25 scale;
   * a 45-s greybox animatic of the lifetime shot;
   * 24 frames of the ink run at 0.5 scale.

   *Fallbacks:*
   * S28: fewer, larger bearers, closer to camera, with real flame shapes.
   * S17: four visits instead of six, letting the cairn and the sky carry time.
   * S21: ink only on the far ranges, with the near terrain photographic and graded warm.

3. **The dread and the dawn don't balance.** The brink could stay abstract (a light show that frightens no one),
   or the flourishing could tip into a poster (utopia, keynote).
   *Test in week 1, with animatics built from stills:*
   * Cut A's brink (S06–S10) to temp music and show it cold, asking "what was about to happen?" If nobody says
     "everything would burn", add weight: a longer plunge, and a held frame of the ember world fully aflame before
     the claw.
   * Cut A's last 40 s twice, once with the valleys as haze and smoke and once as roofs and terraces in clear
     light. Keep the subtlest version that still reads as plenty.

**If only one idea survives this treatment:** the same fire is a glare when you race toward it and a dawn when you
carry it together. Draft 1 made the dawn arrive on its own. v3 makes the fire become the dawn, slowly, in many
hands. Everything else (a night, a lifetime and an age; embers, stone and ink) is how three different films say
that without ever saying it.
