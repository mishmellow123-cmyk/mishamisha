*Independent critic, fresh eyes. 26 Sep 2026, night.*
*Read in full: `treatment_v3.md` @ 29dab8e, `PRODUCERS_VERDICT_DRAFT1.md` @ 444f8fe, `panel_story.md` @ d2f5614,

The treatment is the best writing this project has had. B is close to finished as an idea, A has a real mechanism,
and C finally tells Tolkien's own story. But it still has the producers' four complaints: some of it is on the nose,
parts are incoherent, it is still repackaged, and it has too much in it. It also has one production fact nobody has
priced: **no one in the loop can listen, and no one in the loop is a human viewer.**

## 1. BLOCKERS (ranked; each one would be fatal on its own)

### B1 · A and C are still one plot in two costumes
The producers' "repackaging" note came from watching **A and C**. In v3 those two still share their whole first act,
in the same order and from the same renders:

> A: glyphs → ignition + promise → towers → race → brink → **grasp** → black → **flint → reveal → beacon run** → dawn
> C: glyphs → ignition + promise → towers → race → Eye → **grasp** → black → **flint → reveal → beacon run** → dawn

That is eleven beats from the same shot IDs (E1, E2, E5, E8, H1, R2, R3/R12). The text runs in parallel too, line for
line (see §3). C's difference is a grade and a frame around it, and the treatment's own claim of "no shared running
order" doesn't hold for these two.

**Fix: A keeps the embers act, since it is A's look, and C gives it up.**
- **C's fire is born from the book.** The ink letters on the Red Book's page glow, lift off as sparks and pour into a
  fire that burns through the page (X1, with E1's particles seeded from the page's letter positions: S–M). New line:
  *Ours was kindled from every tale we had ever told.* This cuts C's glyph spiral, its A-style ignition and P3.
- **C keeps only the embers shots A doesn't have:** the Ring forged, gold raining from the Ring, the Eye, and the grasp
  on the Ring.
- **The grasp belongs to C alone** (Isildur). A loses it (B3).
- **C's ink Run gets its own camera.** It travels laterally at beacon height, like a scroll unrolling, instead of
  flying A's glider path. That is only new `rcam` keys.
- Rewrite C's parallel lines (§2).

After this, the one shared beat is her fire and its reveal (H1 + R2). The producers allowed that as "efficient reuse
of key visuals".

### B2 · C's carved vows are a policy charter
> NO SINGLE HAND SHALL HOLD IT · NO FASTER THAN WE CAN SEE · NO FORGE IN THE DARK · WHAT IT GIVES, IT GIVES TO ALL

These are four capitalised imperatives, carved into a round table while delegates of every people look on. They
match the north star's points one to one: power stays distributed, pace until verified, the work made public, shared
benefit. On screen that is a treaty signing, which is the exact thing the producers ruled out: the north star
*recognised*. They also aren't Tolkien, because Elrond's council carved nothing. And the treatment's own "referent"
column shows each vow repeating a picture the film has already shown.

**Fix:** cut C22 (−13 s, and one fewer `accord_C` fix). Use Tolkien's own council image instead ("Bring out the
Ring"). She sets the Ring on the stone at the centre. The camera orbits. Every hand in the circle holds a torch, and
none reaches for it. If a carving has to stay, carve an *answer* in the Ring's own invented script, untranslated,
around the hearth: one inscription answering another. Fans will feel the rhyme, and nobody reads a platform.

### B3 · C's hinge line now contradicts C
> *In the old story, the Ring is unmade in the fire that forged it. / This fire could not be unmade.*

This worked in draft 1, when the fire *was* the danger. v3 splits them ("the fire is not the Ring"), so the line now
answers the wrong question. If the forging fire can't be unmade, it is still there to throw the Ring into, so the
line props the old ending open instead of closing it. The chain in C2 ("so the story needs a new forge") is a non
sequitur on screen, and "metaphorically incoherent" was the producers' word for draft 1.

**Fix:** *In the old story, the Ring is unmade in the fire that forged it. / Ours was forged at every forge at once.*
Now Tolkien's law closes the old ending and dictates the new one. Her lone fire can't touch it (the fire test), so she
must call; only a fire every forge and people light together can unmake it (the council). Forged at every forge,
unmade in the fire everyone lit: the whole of C in one symmetry.

### B4 · A's grasp breaks A's one physics, adds a villain and makes T9 a cheat
A10 has "a claw of embers descends from above… closes over the fire… IMPACT", and then T9 says *There would have been
no morning after it.* The audience has just watched it happen, and the conditional says it didn't. A monster claw
from the sky is also a Dark Lord in the one film that has none, and it is C's image. A6's claim of "one physics" (fire
and sight) fails the moment a hand seizes.

**Fix:** cut E8 from A. A's catastrophe should be its own physics: the rim gives way under the gilded towers, the
crown falls, and the camera tips over the rim into the fire until the frame is white. Then **draw the dead valley in
the PROMISE's own ember technique, grey.** It becomes the fire's second vision, the promise inverted, and T9 is then
exactly right. Also cut E7's ember Earth seen from above the pole. It is the film's one orbital shot of the literal
planet, and the treatment's own rule is "no cut leaves the ground". Saves −10 s, one EXTEND and one re-aim.

### B5 · A and B end on the same shot, and A's version is a poster
- **A23:** "watch-fires paling by their local sun elevation".
- **R11:** "each far beacon pales as the light reaches it".

Both use the same renderer, the same rule, the same range and the same climax. On top of that, A has "the sun's rim
rises exactly behind the lantern… one light" on the highest cairn. That reads as a torch raised to the sunrise, a flag
on a summit: triumph, plus an "AI is the new sun" reading.

**Fix: B owns the sunrise; A ends in the blue hour.**
- The lantern is set down among the watch-fires.
- The east pales to rose.
- The cloud sea thins onto hearth smoke rising from the valley in grey-blue light.
- No sun disc, no paling rule, no alignment.

*No one got there first.* then lands on a dawn that hasn't finished arriving, which is the title taken literally.
It is also cheaper and safer: R7 was rated "high" risk, and with the sun at −6° to −2° there is no limb and no
starburst to get wrong.

### B6 · The towers are nationally coded (on disk now)
On embers_v2 (546–570) and embers_C (592–612) the towers include a **pagoda**, an **obelisk** and a lattice spire. At
570 an obelisk and a pagoda flank the fire. The north star includes a US–China deal, so that pair is the most
decodable image in the project. Both panels checked the staging but not the architecture.

**Fix:** re-dress `towers2.py` in one invented, placeless family of furnace-stacks and forge-towers, varied in height
and girth but never in culture. That also makes "the forges raced" and the bellows literal. S effort on the EMBERS
lane, before any E5, E6, E10 or E11 render.

### B7 · The heroine's face sits at the hinge of all three films, and the slower take shows it longer
H1 is "one slower take for all cuts". On the v2b sheet (1314–1324) the profile lit by the tinder is the face the
producers called "a bit weird": it reads as clay. v3 slows the take down and gives C twenty seconds of it.

**Fix:** re-key the slower take so the face is never lit or in focus. Show the tinder, the gloves, the sparks, her
breath and the scarf, with the head as a rim-lit silhouette at most. Where re-keying costs too much, crop the existing
frames to the hands and tinder (the face sits in the right half). S effort on the HEROINE lane, hour 0.

### B8 · A's literal intrusions: draft 1's "uneven allegory and literal" again
- **M1**, "a city rooftop… the film's one image of now". A modern rooftop in the middle of the myth is the jolt the
  producers named. **Cut M1** (−10 s) and move the ANSWER's first hearing to the Run.
- **R4**, "chains of summit fires close… around the cold glow" in a high wide. Seen from above, a closed ring around a
  glowing site is a cordon diagram ("compute is watched"), whatever the shot notes say. **Cut R4** (−10 s) and show the
  watchers only in eyelines, from behind a watcher looking toward the glow.
- **Four AI signals in twenty seconds:** the glyphs, *We had fed it everything…*, filaments "plainly visible", and
  *And it began to think.* **Cut T3**, and keep the filaments below notice. The breathing that is too even is the
  uncanny cue that does the work.

### B9 · C's Eye and the Ring's fall have no cause, and the Havens has no reason
- **The Eye.** T5 says there was no Dark Lord, and twenty seconds later Sauron's own Eye opens.
  **Fix:** use Tolkien's words for it, "the black slit of its pupil opened on a pit, a window into nothing". The
  *first* time we see the Eye, the slit opens onto empty black. An Eye with no one behind it *is* the race, and it
  fits "no Dark Lord".
- **The fall.** In the book the Ring leaves Isildur because it seeks its master. Here there is no master.
  **Fix:** the claw closes on the Ring and cannot hold it. The crust glows from inside and cracks, and the band slips
  through the fingers. "No single hand can hold it" becomes physics rather than a vow.
- **The Havens.** Why must a young beacon-keeper sail West? In the book the reason is the wound.
  **Fix:** the Havens page shows one drawn detail: her bound hand raising the small light.

### B10 · The fan laughs in C
- **The eagles:** the most-memed thing in the legendarium ("why didn't they fly?"). "Late, small, flying away" still
  invites the joke. **Cut.**
- ***They were not hasty.***: Treebeard's "Don't be hasty" is a meme and will get the chuckle. **Replace** (§2).
- **Round doors on the plenty page:** Hobbiton merchandise. **Cut them.** The silver tree in the field is the
  reference.
- **The cock crow:** keep it only if it is distant and soft under the harp. It is the one deep cut that could land as a
  farmyard.

### B11 · The plan's human gates have no humans
The plan relies on:
- "the producers' first listening" at hour 8 and again at 16;
- "two Tolkien readers and two strangers";
- "cold viewers" for the lifetime test;
- "readers who know the field" for the decoder test.

The producers are away, nobody else is in the loop, and the composer cannot listen. **Every taste gate in §10 has no
judge.**

**Fix:** a fresh-context critic agent at each gate. It gets only the contact sheets, the animatic and the lines, never
the treatment, and answers four fixed questions:
1. Tell the story back.
2. Name any real company, country, deal or institution it brings to mind.
3. Which moment would make an audience laugh?
4. Does any frame read as a doll, clip-art, a poster or a tech demo?

Design the score for no ears (§5).

## 4. C's Tolkien depth

**Verdict:** yes, this is *The Lord of the Rings* throughout, and lovingly. Its spine is the life of a Ring: forged,
seized, lost, found by someone small, tested in the hearth fire, a call for aid, a council, the bearer failing at the
brink, grace, plenty, and the Havens. It has about twenty references, almost all of them deeds and objects rather than
names. The Moria line, the Mirror, Gandalf's fire test, the Red Book's last pages and "the swift sunrise vs the long
dawn" are the work of someone who knows the book. None of it is parody, except the four fan laughs in B10.

**What's wrong:**
1. **The T8 logic** (B3).
2. **The Eye after "no Dark Lord"**, and the Ring's fall with no cause (B9).
3. **The fire test gets Frodo backwards.** At Bag End Frodo *cannot* throw the Ring into the fire; Gandalf does it for
   him. In C she opens her fist at her own fire, then can't at the council. **Fix:** she holds the Ring in her beacon
   on the tip of her steel. It lies there unmarked, letters awake, not even warm, and she cannot let it fall off. That
   is Bag End exactly, and it plants the council.
4. **The vows** (B2). The book's council is the Ring set on the stone where all can see it, and no one reaches.

**What's missing (cheapest first):**
- **Pity, and Gollum's role.** The book's moral hinge is that grace comes through someone once spared. The labs
  framing gives C this for free: *the forges that raced are at the council*, and their torches are part of the fire
  everyone lit. Show one gilded or ember-scarred hand among the torches. The one that grasped helps save it. That is
  "neither heroes nor villains" in Tolkien's deepest key, and costs S (a cloak variant in `accord_C`).
- **The Road.** The book is mostly a journey, and C cuts from her beacon to the council. On `map_C`, while the
  beacons race across the sheet, one slow dotted route crosses it toward the council, like the endpaper maps. That
  pictures "borne", and it also pictures pace without a word. S effort.
- **Saruman,** the wise one who meant to use it well, and the closest analogue to a lab in all of Tolkien.
  *Even the wise said: let us take it first, and use it well.* can replace T6 if the critic agent reads T6's "enemies"
  as geopolitics. Don't add it on top of T6.
- **Music:** the beacon run is the most Peter Jackson thing in the project. Keep the 3+3+2 and the call's contour, so
  it can never be mistaken for the film's beacon cue.

## 5. SCOPE: the plan

**The arithmetic:**
- 10 lanes × 17 h = 170 lane-hours.
- Edit/mix takes 1 lane and music takes 2, leaving ~100 picture lane-hours.
- Gates, hand-offs and queueing cost ~15, leaving ~85.
- Fixes to reused shots cost ~30 (the production panel's 35, less the shots cut below), leaving **~55 lane-hours for
  new builds**. That is 7 to 8 builds of size L.
- The treatment asks for 14 showpieces and ~20 other new shots, which the panel priced at ≈140 lane-hours for this
  slate. **So cut about 60% of the new work.**

Render CPU is not the constraint (12 cloud boxes, ~25 box-hours). **The score is the long pole.**

not because it matters least. It is Misha's cut.

### 5.4 A score for a composer who cannot listen
- **Sparse beats dense.** Double every exposed melodic line or sit it on a pad. B's solo cello and C's cor anglais are
  where a blind sampler score sounds most like MIDI, so double or pad them.
- **No fast ensemble figuration, and percussion stops at sixteenths at 72 BPM.** In A's "doubling every two bars", the
  last bar is a roll, not 32nd-note taiko.
- **C's dawn is warm, not loud, like A's and B's:** sustained strings and horns, the CALL on one horn, a timpani roll
  from pp to mf. Octave violins at full, from samples, is the most cringe-prone sound available.
- **The effects are the floor.** Wind, fire, the strikes, the page and the hearth are synthesised and never sound fake.
- **An analysis battery for every section:**
  - loudness (LUFS) against the level map, and true peak;
  - onsets on the bar grid;
  - no stuck or overlapping notes;
  - no identical sample repeated more than about 8 times a second;
  - the spectral centroid follows the intended arc.
- **A fallback master for each cut by H10:** effects, the drone, the silence piano and the CALL on horns. If a draft
  fails the battery or the critic, ship the fallback. A sparse score can't be cringe; a fake orchestra can.

## 6. Verdict in five lines

1. Three films is right. But A and C are still one plot in two costumes for 3½ minutes, and A ends on B's shot. Fix
   both before anything renders.
2. The worst on-the-nose risks are C's carved vows (a four-point charter on a round table) and A's grasp, ember Earth,
   city rooftop and cordon of fires. Cut them all.
3. C's hinge line now contradicts C's own split between the fire and the Ring. *Ours was forged at every forge at
   once.* makes the finale Tolkien's law instead of a non sequitur.
4. The slate doesn't fit 17 h, 10 lanes and a composer who can't listen. Cut to 13:17 with eight gated showpieces and
   a sparse score, and put a critic agent where the absent humans were supposed to be.
5. B is nearly perfect already, A is one restructure away, and C is the most loved and most at risk, so protect it
   with fallbacks, not ambition.
